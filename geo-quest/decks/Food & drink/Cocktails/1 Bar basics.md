uses them.

## Bar talk
- A drink served unmixed and without ice is served {{neat|how?}} (also "straight up").
- A drink served with ice is served {{on the rocks|how?}}.
- "{{Back up|what phrase?}}" means the same drink again.
- A drink ordered by brand ("a Tanqueray and tonic") is a {{call drink|what kind of drink?}}.
- In American measure, proof is {{twice|how many times?}} the percentage of alcohol: 80 proof is 40% alcohol.
- A {{chaser|what?}} is a non-alcoholic drink taken after a shot, like juice or soda.
- "Soda back" means {{soda on the side|what?}}, most often asked for with scotch.
- "Dry" means {{less sweet|what?}}; on a Champagne label the word for it is {{brut|which word?}}.

## Techniques
- To {{muddle|what verb?}} is to crush an ingredient (mint, sugar, lime) in the bottom of the glass.
- To {{float|what verb?}} is to pour a little liquor carefully on top of a drink, over the back of a spoon.
- To {{frost|what verb?}} a glass is to rub its rim with sugar; a sugar-rimmed glass is called a {{crusta|what?}}.
- To {{color|what verb?}} a drink is to tint it with a few drops of another liquid, like grenadine in a Tequila Sunrise.
- To {{fill|what verb?}} is to top the glass up with the last ingredient, usually the mixer.
- To {{strain|what verb?}} is to pour from the shaker through a strainer, leaving the ice behind.
- To {{decant|what verb?}} is to pour wine off its sediment into another vessel, often a carafe that lets it breathe.
- Shake easy mixes for about {{10 seconds|how long?}}; drinks with egg, syrup or cream for about {{20 seconds|how long?}}.
- Never mix more than {{two|how many?}} cocktails in one shaker.
- Shaking or stirring on ice adds about {{½ oz|how much?}} of water to a drink.
- To salt a rim, moisten it with a {{lemon or lime wedge|what?}}; to frost it, with an {{orange or lime wedge|what?}}.

## Measures
- A dash (or splash) is about {{1/32 oz|how much?}}: a few drops.
- A bar spoon (teaspoon) is {{⅛ oz|how much?}}.
- A pony is {{1 oz (30 mL)|how much?}}.
- A shot, or jigger, is {{1½ oz (45 mL)|how much?}}.
- A fifth, the standard bottle, is {{750 mL (25.4 oz)|how much?}}.
- A magnum is {{1.5 liters|how much?}}: two standard bottles.
- A split, the small Champagne bottle, is {{187 mL|how much?}}.
- A standard cocktail is about {{2 to 3 oz|how much?}}; a spirit served neat or on the rocks, about {{1½ to 2 oz|how much?}}.
- A glass of wine is about {{4 to 5 oz|how much?}}.
- In a free pour, one count is about {{½ oz|how much?}}, so a shot is {{3 counts|how many?}}.
- A half keg of beer pours about {{180|how many?}} 12-ounce glasses.

## Glasses

- The short, wide glass for drinks on the rocks is the {{lowball (old-fashioned) glass|which glass?}}.
- The tall, straight glass for a shot and a mixer is the {{highball (Collins) glass|which glass?}}: 8 to 12 oz.
- A Martini or Margarita glass holds {{4 to 8 oz|how much?}}, so the recipe is scaled to the glass.
- The tiny 1-ounce glass for liqueurs and layered drinks is the {{cordial (pousse-café) glass|which glass?}}.

## Garnishes
- A twist is a strip of {{citrus peel|what?}}, twisted over the drink to spray its oils on the surface.
- Drinks filled with tonic are garnished with {{a lime|what?}}.
- A Gibson is a Martini garnished with {{a pearl onion|what?}}.
- A Martini's olives come in an {{odd|odd or even?}} number.
- A Bloody Mary is garnished with {{a celery stick|what?}} and lime.
- Hot drinks and cream drinks like the Brandy Alexander are topped with a sprinkle of {{nutmeg|which spice?}}.
