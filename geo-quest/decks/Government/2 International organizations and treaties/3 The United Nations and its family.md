## Born from a war
The **League of Nations** (1920) failed to stop the Second World War; the United States never joined it. The **United Nations** was its second try: the Charter was signed in **San Francisco on June 26, 1945** and came into force on **October 24, 1945**, now UN Day. 51 founding members; **193** today, the newest South Sudan (2011). Two observer states sit in without a vote: the **Holy See** and **Palestine**.

## Six principal organs
```org
# The UN Charter's six organs
United Nations
  General Assembly | every member, one vote; New York
  Security Council | 15 members, 5 with a veto; New York
  Secretariat | the staff, led by the Secretary-General
  Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC) | coordinates the agencies
  International Court of Justice | disputes between states; The Hague
  Trusteeship Council | idle since 1994, when Palau, the last trust territory, became independent
```

## The Security Council
Five **permanent members** with a veto (China, France, Russia, the UK and the US: the P5) and **ten elected** for two-year terms. Its resolutions under **Chapter VII** (threats to the peace) bind every member and can authorize sanctions or force. A single P5 "no" kills a resolution; abstaining doesn't.

## The family
Specialized agencies have their own members and budgets: the WHO (health, Geneva), UNESCO (education, science and culture, Paris), the FAO (food, Rome), the IAEA (nuclear, Vienna, technically a related organization), the ILO (labor, Geneva). The **IMF** and **World Bank** (Washington), born at **Bretton Woods** in 1944, are UN agencies too, but weighted by money: the US holds a veto over their biggest decisions.
:::
