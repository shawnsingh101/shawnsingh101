Where the United States stands with the big international bodies and treaties today: what it has left, what has lapsed, and what is being decided. A live topic: these cards are checked every Wednesday and rewritten when things change. Neutral and attributed: each side's reasons in its own words.

## A year of exits
In January 2025 the new administration announced it would leave the **World Health Organization** and the **Paris Agreement**, and stop taking part in the UN **Human Rights Council**. Both withdrawals took effect in January 2026, a year after notice, as the treaties require. In July 2025 it announced it would leave **UNESCO** again (it had left in 2018 and rejoined in 2023), effective at the end of 2026. In January 2026 a presidential memorandum ordered withdrawal from 66 more international organizations, among them the UN climate convention (UNFCCC). The administration's stated reason: bodies that, in its words, work against American interests and waste taxpayers' money. Critics, including many former officials, argue that leaving cedes influence to China.

## A lapse
**New START**, the last treaty limiting US and Russian strategic nuclear weapons, expired on **February 5, 2026**. Russia had offered to keep its limits for a year if the US did the same; the US, saying any new deal should include China, gave no formal answer.

## A choice
**António Guterres**'s second term as UN Secretary-General ends on **December 31, 2026**. By custom it is Latin America's turn, and many members want the first woman. The Security Council has been holding straw polls since July 2026; the P5's veto decides.
