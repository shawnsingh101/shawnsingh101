The treaties that shaped the world, from the Peace of Westphalia to the Paris Agreement: what each settled and when.

## Making the modern state system
- The 1648 peace that ended the Thirty Years' War and is said to have founded the system of sovereign states is the Peace of {{Westphalia|what?}}.
native: Westfälischer Friede (German)
related: Germany
- The Peace of Westphalia was signed in two cities, Osnabrück and {{Münster|which city?}}.
native: Münster (German)
- The 1494 treaty in which Spain and Portugal divided the newly found lands outside Europe along a line in the Atlantic is the Treaty of {{Tordesillas|what?}}.
native: Tratado de Tordesillas (Spanish)
related: Spain, Portugal, Brazil
- The 1814–15 conference that redrew Europe after Napoleon was the Congress of {{Vienna|which city?}}.
native: Wiener Kongress (German)
related: Austria

## America's treaties
- The 1783 treaty in which Britain recognized American independence is the Treaty of {{Paris|which city?}}.
related: United Kingdom
- The 1803 treaty that doubled the size of the United States is the {{Louisiana Purchase|what?}}.
related: France, Louisiana
- The 1848 treaty that ended the Mexican–American War and ceded California and the Southwest is the Treaty of {{Guadalupe Hidalgo|what?}}.
native: Tratado de Guadalupe Hidalgo (Spanish)
related: Mexico, California
- The 1867 treaty by which the US bought Alaska was signed with {{Russia|which country?}}.
related: Alaska, Russia

## War and peace in the 20th century
- The 1919 treaty that ended the First World War with Germany is the Treaty of {{Versailles|what?}}.
native: Traité de Versailles (French)
related: France, Germany
- Article 231 of the Treaty of Versailles, blaming Germany for the war, is known as the {{war guilt|what?}} clause.
- The 1928 pact in which countries renounced war as a tool of policy is the {{Kellogg–Briand Pact|what?}}.
- The four Geneva Conventions, the core of the laws of war, were adopted in {{1949|what year?}}; the first convention dates from 1864.
related: Switzerland
- The North Atlantic Treaty, founding NATO, was signed in {{Washington|which city?}} on April 4, 1949.
- The heart of the North Atlantic Treaty, "an armed attack against one… shall be considered an attack against them all", is Article {{5|which article?}}.
- NATO's Article 5 has been invoked once, after the attacks of {{September 11, 2001|when?}}.
- The 1955 Soviet answer to NATO was the {{Warsaw Pact|what?}}, dissolved in 1991.
native: Варшавский договор Varshavskiy dogovor (Russian)

## Building Europe
- The 1957 treaty that created the European Economic Community is the Treaty of {{Rome|which city?}}.
native: Trattato di Roma (Italian)
- The 1992 treaty that created the European Union and set up the euro is the Treaty of {{Maastricht|which city?}}.
native: Verdrag van Maastricht (Dutch)
related: Netherlands
- The 2007 treaty that gave the EU its current structure, and Article 50 for leaving, is the Treaty of {{Lisbon|which city?}}.
native: Tratado de Lisboa (Portuguese)

## Arms control
- The 1968 treaty in which countries without nuclear weapons promise not to get them is the {{Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty|what?}} (NPT).
- The four UN members that have never joined the NPT are India, Pakistan, Israel and {{South Sudan|which country?}}; North Korea announced its withdrawal in 2003.
- The 1987 Reagan–Gorbachev treaty that scrapped a whole class of missiles is the {{INF Treaty (Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces)|which treaty?}}; the US left it in 2019.
- The last US–Russia treaty limiting strategic nuclear weapons, signed in 2010, was {{New START|which treaty?}}.
- New START expired on February {{5|what day?}}, 2026, leaving no treaty limits on the two largest nuclear arsenals for the first time since 1972.
- The 1967 treaty that bans nuclear weapons in orbit and claims of sovereignty on the Moon is the {{Outer Space Treaty|what?}}.
- The 1959 treaty that keeps a whole continent for peace and science, and freezes territorial claims there, is the {{Antarctic Treaty|what?}}.
related: Antarctica
- The 1997 treaty banning anti-personnel landmines, which the US never joined, is the {{Ottawa Treaty|what?}}.
related: Canada

## The planet
- The 1987 treaty that phased out the chemicals destroying the ozone layer is the {{Montreal Protocol|what?}}, often called the most successful environmental treaty.
related: Canada
- The 1997 climate treaty that set binding targets for rich countries only is the {{Kyoto Protocol|what?}}.
native: 京都議定書 Kyōto Giteisho (Japanese)
related: Japan
- The 2015 climate treaty in which every country sets its own target is the {{Paris Agreement|what?}}.
- The Paris Agreement aims to hold warming well below {{2 °C|how much?}}, and to try for 1.5 °C.
- The 1982 treaty that sets 12-nautical-mile territorial seas and 200-mile exclusive economic zones is the {{UN Convention on the Law of the Sea|what?}} (UNCLOS), which the US follows but has never ratified.
- An exclusive economic zone reaches {{200|how many?}} nautical miles from the coast.
