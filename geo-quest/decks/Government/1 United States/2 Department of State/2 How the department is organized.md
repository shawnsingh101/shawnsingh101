The org chart: the Secretary and two deputies, six under secretaries known by their letters, the bureaus under each, and the offices that report straight to the top.

:::lesson
## The shape of it
State is a pyramid with five tiers of senior leaders, every one of them nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate down to assistant secretary:

```stack
# The chain of command
S | Secretary of State | runs the department, advises the President
D | Deputy Secretary | the number two; D-MR, the Deputy for Management and Resources, is number three
P, E, F, M, R, T | Under secretaries | six, each over a family of bureaus
Bureaus | Assistant secretaries | each runs a bureau, with deputy assistant secretaries under them
Offices | Office directors and desk officers | the working level; a desk officer follows one country
```

Every office has a **symbol**: S for the Secretary, D for the deputy, a letter for each under secretary, and initials for each bureau (AF, EUR, CA…). Insiders use them constantly: "send it to P", "clear it with L".

## The six under secretaries
| Letter | Under Secretary for | What their bureaus do |
|---|---|---|
| P | Political Affairs | the six regional bureaus and International Organization Affairs: day-to-day relations with every country |
| E | Economic Affairs | trade, energy, business, the environment and science, cyberspace and digital policy |
| F | Foreign Assistance, Humanitarian Affairs and Religious Freedom | foreign aid, disasters and humanitarian response, refugees and migration, global health, human rights |
| M | Management | the department's machinery: consular affairs, diplomatic security, buildings, people, budget, technology, medical care |
| R | Public Diplomacy and Public Affairs | explaining US policy to the world, and educational and cultural exchanges such as Fulbright |
| T | Arms Control and International Security | arms control, nonproliferation, and political-military affairs such as arms sales |

**P** has traditionally been the most senior, and is often filled by a career diplomat: it runs the regional bureaus that handle relations country by country. **F** is new: the 2025 reorganization created it when USAID's programs moved into State, and it replaced the old J (Civilian Security, Democracy and Human Rights).

## Regional and functional bureaus
**Regional bureaus** (under P) each cover part of the world, and every embassy reports to one: African Affairs (AF), East Asian and Pacific Affairs (EAP), European and Eurasian Affairs (EUR), Near Eastern Affairs (NEA), South and Central Asian Affairs (SCA), Western Hemisphere Affairs (WHA). **International Organization Affairs (IO)**, also under P, handles the UN and other international bodies.

**Functional bureaus** (under the other under secretaries) each cover a subject worldwide. A few that come up constantly:
- **Consular Affairs (CA)**: passports, visas, Americans in trouble abroad. Under M.
- **Diplomatic Security (DS)**: protects people, posts and information; also a federal law enforcement agency. Under M.
- **Overseas Buildings Operations (OBO)**: builds and maintains embassies. Under M.
- **Political-Military Affairs (PM)**: the link with the Pentagon, and arms sales and export licenses. Under T.
- **Democracy, Human Rights, and Labor (DRL)** and **Population, Refugees, and Migration (PRM)**: under F.
- **Educational and Cultural Affairs (ECA)**: exchanges like Fulbright. Under R.

## Reporting straight to the Secretary
Some offices sit outside the under secretaries:
- **Policy Planning Staff (S/P)**: the in-house think tank, created in 1947; its first director was George Kennan, author of containment.
- **Legal Adviser (L)**: the department's lawyers, on treaties and international law.
- **Intelligence and Research (INR)**: State's own intelligence analysts, part of the US Intelligence Community.
- **Legislative Affairs (H)**: relations with Congress.
- **Office of Inspector General (OIG)**: independent auditors and investigators.
- **Executive Secretariat (S/ES)**: moves paper to and from the Secretary; its **Operations Center** watches the world around the clock.
- **Chief of Protocol**: state visits, and the rules of diplomatic rank.

## How a country fits in
Every country has a **desk** in its regional bureau: Kenya is followed by the Kenya desk, in an office of the Bureau of African Affairs, under the Under Secretary for Political Affairs. Cables from the embassy in Nairobi go to that desk, and instructions go back out through it.
