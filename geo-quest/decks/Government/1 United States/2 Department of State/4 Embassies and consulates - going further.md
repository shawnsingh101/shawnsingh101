Situations an embassy or the department actually faces: say which section, bureau or office takes it, and why.

## At the embassy
- A US citizen's passport is stolen in Rome: the embassy's {{consular section|who helps?}} issues an emergency passport.
- An American dies while traveling abroad: the family is helped by the consular unit called American {{Citizen Services|what?}}.
- The host country's parliament is debating a law on foreign NGOs, and Washington wants analysis: the {{political section|who writes it?}} reports by cable.
- A US company says it's being treated unfairly in a host country tender: the {{economic section|which section?}} and the Commerce Department's Foreign Commercial Service take it up.
- A local newspaper runs a false story about US policy: the {{public diplomacy section|which section?}} responds.
- The embassy's lease, housing for staff and local hiring are run by the {{management section|which section?}}.
- A credible threat against the embassy arrives overnight: the first call is to the {{regional security officer|whom?}}.
- A local employee of twenty years is the one who knows which ministry official really decides: that's the value of {{locally employed staff|whom?}}.

## Between governments
- Washington wants the host government told, formally, that it objects to a vote at the UN: the embassy delivers a {{démarche|what?}}.
- A US diplomat is caught spying and the host government wants them gone: it declares them {{persona non grata|what?}}.
- A diplomat's car hits a pedestrian and the host country wants to prosecute: only the {{sending country|which?}} can waive immunity.
- Police arrest a US tourist and never tell the embassy: the host country broke the rule on consular {{notification|what?}}.
- The ambassador is recalled for consultations, and the embassy is run meanwhile by the {{chargé d'affaires|whom?}}.

## In Washington
- A US company wants to export night-vision equipment: it needs a license from the Directorate of Defense Trade Controls, in the Bureau of {{Political-Military Affairs|which bureau?}}.
- A foreign army unit credibly accused of torture asks for US training: the {{Leahy|which?}} Law bars it.
- A treaty needs the Senate's consent: the department's Bureau of {{Legislative Affairs|which bureau?}} works with the Hill.
- A question on whether an agreement binds the US under international law goes to the Office of the {{Legal Adviser|which office?}}.
- An officer strongly disagrees with a policy and wants it on record: they use the {{Dissent Channel|what?}}.
- A hurricane devastates a Caribbean country and US help is needed fast: since 2025 that falls to F, through its Bureau of Disaster and {{Humanitarian Response|what?}}.
