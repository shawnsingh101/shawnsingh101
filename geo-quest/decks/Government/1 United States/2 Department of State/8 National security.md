## The National Security Council
The **National Security Act of 1947** built the post-war security system: it created the **National Security Council**, the Central Intelligence Agency, and what became the Department of Defense. The NSC is the President's forum for national security decisions. By law its members include the President, the Vice President, the Secretaries of **State**, **Defense** and **Energy**; the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff and the Director of National Intelligence advise it. Its staff, in the White House, is run by the **national security adviser**, a presidential assistant who, unlike the Secretary of State, is not confirmed by the Senate.

In May 2025, Secretary Rubio was also made national security adviser, the first person to hold both jobs at once since Henry Kissinger in the 1970s.

## How the agencies decide
Big questions climb a ladder of committees, so each level settles what it can and sends up only real disagreements:

```stack
# The interagency ladder
NSC | National Security Council | the President decides
PC | Principals Committee | cabinet secretaries, chaired by the national security adviser
DC | Deputies Committee | their deputies, chaired by the deputy national security adviser
PCC | Policy Coordination Committees | assistant secretaries and senior directors, by region or issue
```

State sits at every level: the Secretary at the Principals Committee, the Deputy Secretary or an under secretary at the Deputies, assistant secretaries and their staff at the Policy Coordination Committees.

## The National Security Strategy
By law (the Goldwater-Nichols Act of 1986), the President sends Congress a **National Security Strategy**. In practice each administration issues one or two. It sets the priorities the departments then turn into their own strategies, like the Defense Department's National Defense Strategy.

The **2025 National Security Strategy**, dated November 2025, broke with its predecessors. In its own terms:
- The **Western Hemisphere** comes first among regions. The goal is a hemisphere stable and well-governed enough to prevent mass migration to the US, governments that work with the US against cartels, and no hostile foreign control of key assets there. It calls this a "**Trump Corollary**" to the Monroe Doctrine, with the motto "**Enlist and Expand**".
- **China**'s growing presence in the hemisphere is to be countered, and critical supply chains brought closer to home.
- The **Middle East** matters less than before, it says, because of greater US energy independence.
- Allies are expected to carry more of the burden of their own defense. At NATO's 2025 summit, members agreed to raise defense spending toward **5% of GDP** by 2035.

At State, Secretary Rubio puts the same idea as a test for every dollar, program and policy: does it make America **safer, stronger, or more prosperous**?
