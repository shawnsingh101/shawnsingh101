title: The money
---
How diplomacy is paid for: the international affairs budget, the yearly bill that funds State, the difference between authorizing and appropriating, and the cuts of 2025 and 2026.

:::lesson
## A small slice
Everything the US spends on foreign affairs, from embassies to foreign aid to UN dues, is a little more than **1%** of the federal budget. In the federal budget's numbering it's **Function 150**, "international affairs", so people in Washington speak of "the 150 account". Defense spending is many times larger.

## Authorize, then appropriate
Congress funds things in two steps:
- An **authorization** law creates or changes a program and says how much it *may* get. For State, that's the job of the Senate Foreign Relations and House Foreign Affairs committees.
- An **appropriation** law actually provides the money, each year. That's the job of the **Appropriations** committees. Without it, nothing is spent, whatever was authorized.

## The yearly bill
State, foreign aid and related agencies are funded in one of the twelve annual appropriations bills. For decades it was called **State, Foreign Operations, and Related Programs** (SFOPS), and the Senate still uses that name. In 2025 the House renamed its version **National Security, Department of State, and Related Programs** (NSRP).

For fiscal year 2026, it was enacted on **February 3, 2026**, as Division F of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026: about **50 billion dollars**, roughly 16% less than in 2025.

Two kinds of money run through it:
- **Diplomatic operations**: salaries, embassies, security, IT, the running of the department.
- **Foreign assistance**: aid, health programs, humanitarian relief, security assistance to other countries.

The bureau that manages the department's own budget sits under the Under Secretary for **Management**; the foreign aid budget is overseen under **F**.

## Fees
Not all of it is appropriated. Consular fees, such as what visa applicants pay, fund much of the consular work that collects them.

## Rescissions
Congress can also take back money already appropriated, with a **rescission** law. In July 2025 it passed one cancelling about **9 billion dollars**, most of it foreign aid; the rest was funding for public broadcasting.
