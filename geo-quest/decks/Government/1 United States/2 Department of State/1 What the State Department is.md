---
The State Department in one view: what it does, where it came from, where it sits in the government, and how big it is.

:::lesson
## The lead foreign affairs agency
The State Department is the United States' foreign ministry. Within the executive branch it is the **lead foreign affairs agency**, and the Secretary of State is the President's principal adviser on foreign policy. Its work:
- **representing** the United States abroad, through some 270 embassies, consulates and missions;
- **negotiating** treaties and agreements;
- **protecting Americans** abroad and issuing **passports**;
- deciding who gets a **visa** to come to the US (though at the border, the Department of Homeland Security decides who is admitted);
- running most **foreign assistance**: since July 1, 2025, including the programs that USAID used to run;
- **reporting** back on what's happening in other countries, and keeping the public informed about US policy.

## The first department
Congress created the **Department of Foreign Affairs** in July 1789, the first executive department under the new Constitution. Two months later it was renamed the **Department of State**, because it was also given domestic jobs: keeping the laws, the records and the **Great Seal of the United States**, which it still keeps. The first Secretary of State was **Thomas Jefferson**. A trace of that domestic role survives: a President who resigns does it by letter to the Secretary of State, as Nixon did to Henry Kissinger in 1974.

## Where it sits
- The Secretary is **first in the cabinet's order of precedence**, and **fourth in the line of succession** to the presidency, after the Vice President, the Speaker of the House and the President pro tempore of the Senate.
- Headquarters is the **Harry S Truman Building**, in the **Foggy Bottom** neighborhood of Washington, a few blocks from the White House. "Foggy Bottom" is shorthand for the department, the way "the Pentagon" is for Defense. The Secretary's offices are on the **seventh floor**, which is shorthand for the department's leadership.

## Its size
- Relations with nearly every country. The exceptions with no formal relations include **Iran**, **North Korea** and **Bhutan**. With **Taiwan**, relations are unofficial, run through the **American Institute in Taiwan**, a nonprofit.
- The foreign affairs budget is little more than **1%** of the federal budget.
- Its workforce has three parts: the **Foreign Service** (who serve abroad and rotate), the **Civil Service** (mostly in Washington), and **locally employed staff** hired in each country, the largest group.
