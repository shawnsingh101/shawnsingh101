The seventeen amendments since the Bill of Rights, in four waves: fixes to the founding design, the Reconstruction amendments, the Progressive Era, and the 20th century's reforms of voting and the presidency.

:::lesson
## Four waves
| Wave | Amendments | What they did |
|---|---|---|
| Early fixes | 11 (1795), 12 (1804) | states can't be sued by other states' citizens in federal court; separate electoral votes for President and Vice President |
| Reconstruction | 13 (1865), 14 (1868), 15 (1870) | slavery abolished; citizenship, due process and equal protection; no denying the vote by race |
| Progressive Era | 16 (1913), 17 (1913), 18 (1919), 19 (1920) | income tax; senators elected by the people; Prohibition; women's suffrage |
| 20th century | 20 (1933) to 26 (1971) | new term dates; Prohibition repealed; two-term limit; votes for DC; no poll taxes; disability and succession; voting at 18 |

The **27th** (1992), on congressional pay, is the odd one out: proposed with the Bill of Rights in 1789, it was ratified 203 years later after a University of Texas student, Gregory Watson, revived it in a paper (he got a C).

The **14th** is the most cited: its **citizenship clause** makes everyone born or naturalized here a citizen, its **due process** and **equal protection** clauses bind the states, and through it most of the Bill of Rights applies to them. *Brown v. Board of Education* (1954) rests on its equal protection clause.

The **21st** is the only amendment that repeals another (the 18th), and the only one ratified by state conventions instead of legislatures.
:::

## Reconstruction
- The three Reconstruction amendments
```parts
Slavery abolished :: 13th
Citizenship, due process, equal protection :: 14th
No denying the vote by race :: 15th
```

- The amendment that makes everyone born in the US a citizen and guarantees equal protection of the laws is the {{14th|which?}}, ratified in 1868.
- The amendment that bars denying the vote because of race is the {{15th|which?}}, ratified in 1870.
- The 1954 school desegregation case decided under the 14th Amendment's equal protection clause is {{Brown v. Board of Education|which case?}}.

## Progressive Era
- The amendment allowing a federal income tax is the {{16th|which?}}, ratified in 1913.
- The amendment that has senators elected by the people, not state legislatures, is the {{17th|which?}}, ratified in 1913.
- Prohibition of alcohol came with the {{18th|which?}} Amendment, in 1919.
- Women won the right to vote nationwide with the {{19th|which?}} Amendment, in 1920.
- The four Progressive Era amendments
```parts
Income tax :: 16th
Senators elected by the people :: 17th
Prohibition :: 18th
Women's suffrage :: 19th
```
