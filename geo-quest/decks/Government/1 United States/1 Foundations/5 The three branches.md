The federal government as one chart: Congress, the President and the courts, what sits under each, who leads them now, and the order of succession. Each department of government gets its own section later, starting with State and Defense.

:::lesson
## The chart
```org
# The federal government
The Constitution
  Legislative: Congress | Article I · makes the laws
    Senate | 100 senators, two per state, six-year terms
    House of Representatives | 435 members by population, two-year terms
    Agencies of Congress | Library of Congress, GAO, CBO, Government Publishing Office
  Executive: the President | Article II · carries out the laws
    Vice President | also President of the Senate
    Executive Office of the President | White House staff, National Security Council, OMB
    15 executive departments | their heads form the Cabinet
    Independent agencies | CIA, NASA, the Federal Reserve, the EPA…
  Judicial: the Supreme Court | Article III · interprets the laws
    Courts of appeals | 13 circuits
    District courts | 94, where federal trials happen
```

## How each checks the others
- The **President** can **veto** bills; **Congress** can **override** with two-thirds of each house.
- The **Senate** confirms judges, ambassadors and Cabinet officers, and consents to treaties.
- **Congress** controls the money, can **impeach** and remove officers and judges, and sets the size of the courts.
- The **courts** can strike down laws and executive actions as unconstitutional (judicial review); the President nominates the judges.

## Who can serve
| Office | Minimum age | Citizen for | Term |
|---|---|---|---|
| Representative | 25 | 7 years | 2 years |
| Senator | 30 | 9 years | 6 years (a third up every two years) |
| President | 35 | natural-born; 14 years' residence | 4 years, two elected terms at most |
| Federal judge | none | none | "during good Behaviour": for life |

## Congress
- The Senate has {{100|how many?}} members, two from each state.
- The House of Representatives has {{435|how many?}} voting members.
- A senator's term is {{six|how many?}} years; a representative's is two.
- The House is led by the {{Speaker|what title?}}, who is second in line for the presidency after the Vice President.
- The Vice President's constitutional job in Congress is {{President of the Senate|what title?}}, casting a vote only to break a tie.
- When the Vice President is away, the Senate is led by the President {{pro tempore|what?}}, by custom its longest-serving majority senator.
native: pro tempore (Latin)
- Only the {{House|which chamber?}} can start a bill to raise revenue.
- Congress's nonpartisan scorekeeper for the cost of bills is the {{Congressional Budget Office|which agency?}} (CBO).

## The executive
- The President's term is {{four|how many?}} years.
- The heads of the {{15|how many?}} executive departments make up the core of the Cabinet.
- The oldest executive department, created in July 1789 as the Department of Foreign Affairs, is the Department of {{State|which?}}.
also in: What the State Department is
- The newest executive department, created in 2002 after the September 11 attacks, is the Department of {{Homeland Security|which?}}.
- The White House office that writes the President's budget is the {{Office of Management and Budget|which office?}} (OMB).

## The courts
- The Supreme Court has {{nine|how many?}} justices, a number set by Congress, not the Constitution, since 1869.
- Federal judges serve "during good Behaviour", which in practice means {{for life|how long?}}.
- There are {{13|how many?}} federal courts of appeals: 11 numbered circuits, the D.C. Circuit and the Federal Circuit.
- Federal trials happen in the {{94|how many?}} district courts.
- The Supreme Court hears only about 60 of the thousands of cases it's asked to; agreeing to hear one is granting a writ of {{certiorari|what?}}.
native: certiorari (Latin)

## Who can serve
- The minimum age for the House is {{25|what age?}}; for the Senate, {{30|what age?}}; for the presidency, {{35|what age?}}.
- Only a {{natural-born|what kind of?}} citizen can be President.

## The line of succession
- The line of succession, top of the list
```parts
First in line :: the Vice President
Leads the House :: the Speaker of the House
Leads the Senate when the VP is away :: the President pro tempore
The oldest department :: the Secretary of State
The money :: the Secretary of the Treasury
```
