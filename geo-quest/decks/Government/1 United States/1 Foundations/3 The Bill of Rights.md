## A promise kept
The Anti-Federalists' strongest argument against the Constitution was that it had no list of rights. Several states ratified only on the promise of one. In the First Congress, **James Madison**, who had once thought a bill of rights unnecessary, proposed amendments in **June 1789**; Congress sent **twelve** to the states, and ten were ratified on **December 15, 1791**: the **Bill of Rights**. Of the other two, one, on congressional pay, finally became the 27th Amendment in 1992.

## What they protect
| | |
|---|---|
| 1st | religion (no establishment, free exercise), speech, press, assembly, petition |
| 2nd | to keep and bear arms |
| 3rd | no soldiers quartered in homes in peacetime |
| 4th | no unreasonable searches and seizures; warrants need probable cause |
| 5th | grand juries, no double jeopardy, no forced self-incrimination, due process, just compensation |
| 6th | in criminal trials: speedy and public, an impartial jury, a lawyer, confronting witnesses |
| 7th | jury trials in civil suits over $20 |
| 8th | no excessive bail or fines, no cruel and unusual punishment |
| 9th | rights not listed are still kept by the people |
| 10th | powers not given to the nation are reserved to the states or the people |

At first the Bill of Rights bound only the federal government (*Barron v. Baltimore*, 1833). Since the 14th Amendment (1868), the Supreme Court has applied most of it to the states too, one right at a time: **incorporation**.

## The First Amendment
- The First Amendment's five freedoms
```parts
# any order
Worship, and no state church :: religion
Say it :: speech
Print it :: press
Gather :: assembly
Ask the government to fix it :: petition
```
- The First Amendment's ban on a state church is the {{Establishment|which?}} Clause; the right to practice your faith is the Free Exercise Clause.

## The rights of the accused
- The Fourth Amendment says a warrant must be backed by {{probable cause|what?}}.
- Being tried twice for the same crime, banned by the Fifth Amendment, is {{double jeopardy|what?}}.
- The Fifth Amendment requires {{just compensation|what?}} when the government takes private property for public use.
- "Nor shall any person… be deprived of life, liberty, or property, without {{due process of law|what?}}" (Fifth Amendment).
- The 1966 case that requires police to warn suspects of their right to remain silent is {{Miranda v. Arizona|which case?}}.
- The 1963 case holding that the states must give a lawyer to defendants who can't afford one is {{Gideon v. Wainwright|which case?}}.
- The Fifth Amendment requires a {{grand jury|what?}} to indict for serious federal crimes.
- The Sixth Amendment's trial rights
```parts
# any order
Not delayed, not secret :: speedy and public
Twelve neighbors, fair-minded :: impartial jury
Someone to argue for you :: counsel
Face your accusers :: confront witnesses
```
