title: How the Defense Department is organized
icon: layers
---
Two chains run through the Pentagon: one that **commands** (President, Secretary, combatant commanders) and one that **builds** the force (the military departments and their chiefs). The org charts below show both, and the 11 combatant commands that do the fighting.

:::lesson
## Two chains
```org
# Who commands operations
President | commander in chief
  Secretary of Defense (Secretary of War) | the only civilian link
    Combatant commanders | 11 four-star officers who run operations
```
```org
# Who builds the force: organize, train, equip
Secretary of Defense (Secretary of War)
  Deputy Secretary
  Office of the Secretary of Defense | six under secretaries
  Joint Chiefs of Staff | the top officers; advise, don't command
  Department of the Army | Army
  Department of the Navy | Navy and Marine Corps
  Department of the Air Force | Air Force and Space Force
```
Each military department is run by a civilian **secretary** (Secretary of the Army, of the Navy, of the Air Force), below the Secretary of Defense and outside the Cabinet. The **Joint Chiefs of Staff** are the Chairman, the Vice Chairman, and the chiefs of the six services in the department plus the National Guard. The **Chairman** is the highest-ranking officer and the President's principal military adviser, but by law commands no forces.

## Under the Secretary
The **Office of the Secretary of Defense** has six under secretaries: **Policy**; **Acquisition and Sustainment** (buying weapons); **Research and Engineering**; the **Comptroller** (the money); **Personnel and Readiness**; and **Intelligence and Security**. Defense agencies sit below them, among them the **NSA** (signals intelligence), the **DIA** (military intelligence), **DARPA** (research: it funded the internet's forerunner, ARPANET) and the **Defense Logistics Agency**.

## The combatant commands
Seven cover a part of the world or space, four a function:

| Area | Headquarters | Function | Headquarters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Africa (AFRICOM) | Stuttgart, Germany | Cyber (CYBERCOM) | Fort Meade, Maryland |
| Central, the Middle East (CENTCOM) | Tampa, Florida | Special Operations (SOCOM) | Tampa, Florida |
| Europe (EUCOM) | Stuttgart, Germany | Strategic, nuclear forces (STRATCOM) | Offutt AFB, Nebraska |
| Indo-Pacific (INDOPACOM) | Honolulu, Hawaii | Transportation (TRANSCOM) | Scott AFB, Illinois |
| Northern, home defense (NORTHCOM) | Colorado Springs | | |
| Southern, Latin America (SOUTHCOM) | Doral, Florida | | |
| Space (SPACECOM) | being moved to Huntsville, Alabama | | |

The commander of EUCOM is also NATO's **Supreme Allied Commander Europe** (SACEUR), always an American.
