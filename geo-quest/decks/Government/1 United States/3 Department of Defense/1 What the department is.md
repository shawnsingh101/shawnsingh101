The Department of Defense, which since September 2025 also calls itself the Department of War: where it came from, what it does, and the building it runs from. From the department's own history pages, the National Security Act of 1947 and Executive Order 14347 (public domain).

## From War to Defense, and back
Congress created the **Department of War** on **August 7, 1789**, to run the Army; **Henry Knox** was the first Secretary of War. A separate **Department of the Navy** followed in **1798**. For 150 years the two were rivals with no one above them but the President.

The **National Security Act of 1947**, written after the Second World War, put them under one roof, the *National Military Establishment*, led by the first **Secretary of Defense, James Forrestal**; it also made the **Air Force** a service of its own and created the CIA and the National Security Council. In **1949** the establishment was renamed the **Department of Defense**, and the Army, Navy and Air Force departments lost their Cabinet seats.

The **Goldwater–Nichols Act of 1986** set today's chain of command: orders run from the **President** to the **Secretary of Defense** to the **combatant commanders** who fight. The service chiefs train and equip; they don't command operations.

On **September 5, 2025**, **Executive Order 14347** authorized "Department of War" and "Secretary of War" as secondary titles, used by the administration since. The legal name stays the Department of Defense until Congress changes it by law.

## What it is today
The largest employer in the United States: about **1.3 million** active-duty service members, about 800,000 in the Guard and Reserve, and about 750,000 civilians, with a budget of roughly **$850 billion** a year, about half of all federal discretionary spending. It holds five of the six armed services: the **Army**, **Navy**, **Marine Corps**, **Air Force** and **Space Force** (created December 20, 2019). The sixth, the **Coast Guard**, belongs to Homeland Security in peacetime and can move to the Navy in war.

## The Pentagon
Its headquarters in **Arlington, Virginia**, across the Potomac from Washington: ground was broken on **September 11, 1941**, and it was finished in 16 months. Five sides, five floors above ground, 17.5 miles of corridors, and about 27,000 people. On September 11, 2001, exactly 60 years after work began, American Airlines Flight 77 struck its west side, killing 184 people.
