## Half lit, always
The Moon makes no light; it reflects sunlight. At every moment **half of it is lit**, the half facing the Sun. What changes over a month is **how much of that lit half faces us**, as the Moon circles Earth. The phases are not Earth's shadow: that's a lunar eclipse, and it is rare.

## One cycle
```stack
# From new to new, about 29.5 days
New | same side of the sky as the Sun | rises and sets with the Sun; invisible
Waxing crescent | a sliver, growing | seen in the west after sunset
First quarter | half the disk, a week in | rises about noon, sets about midnight
Waxing gibbous | more than half, growing | "gibbous": humped
Full | opposite the Sun | rises at sunset, highest at midnight, sets at sunrise
Waning gibbous → third quarter → waning crescent | the same, in reverse | third quarter rises about midnight
```
*Waxing* means growing, *waning* shrinking. **Quarter** names where the Moon is in its orbit (a quarter or three-quarters of the way round), not how much is lit: at both quarters we see **half** the disk. In the Northern Hemisphere a waxing Moon is lit on the **right**.

## Two kinds of month
The Moon goes once around Earth relative to the stars in **27.3 days** (the **sidereal month**). But Earth is moving around the Sun meanwhile, so the Moon needs about two more days to line up with the Sun again: phases repeat every **29.5 days** (the **synodic** or solar month). Moving east about 12° a day, the Moon rises on average about **50 minutes later** each day.

## The same face
The Moon spins on its axis once per orbit, so it always turns the same face to Earth: **synchronous rotation**, the result of **tidal locking** by Earth's gravity. The far side isn't dark: it gets as much sunlight as the near side, and is fully lit at new moon. It was first photographed by the Soviet probe **Luna 3** in 1959.

```parts
Invisible, beside the Sun :: new moon
```

```parts
Humped, shrinking :: waning gibbous
Half lit again, three weeks in :: third quarter
A shrinking sliver, before dawn :: waning crescent
Back beside the Sun :: new moon
```

## Two months
- The Moon's orbit measured against the stars, 27.3 days, is the {{sidereal|which?}} month.
- The cycle of phases, from full to full, takes about {{29.5 days|how long?}}.
- The month of phases, set by the Sun, is called the {{synodic|which?}} month.
- The cycle of phases takes longer than one orbit because Earth is moving around the {{Sun|what?}} at the same time.

## The same face
- The Moon spins once on its axis in the same time it takes to orbit Earth, which is called {{synchronous rotation|what?}}.
- Earth's gravity slowed the Moon's spin until it kept one face toward us, a state called {{tidal locking|what?}}.
- The far side of the Moon gets {{as much sunlight|how much?}} as the near side; it is "dark" only in being unseen.
- At new moon, the Moon's {{far side|which side?}} is fully lit.
- The first photographs of the far side were taken in 1959 by the Soviet probe {{Luna 3|which probe?}}.
