The rules an organization sets itself, the laws and standards it must meet, and the rights people have over their data.

:::lesson
## The paperwork, from broad to specific
- A **policy** says what must be done and why (passwords must be strong). Approved by leadership.
- A **standard** makes it measurable (at least 14 characters).
- A **procedure** says how, step by step.
- A **guideline** advises, but isn't required.

Common policies: the **acceptable use policy (AUP)**, what staff may do with company systems; **onboarding and offboarding**; **mandatory vacation** and **job rotation**, which bring hidden fraud to light; **change management**, so no change goes in without review and a way back.

## Laws and standards
- **GDPR** (the EU's General Data Protection Regulation): personal data of people in the EU, wherever the company is. Fines up to 4% of global turnover.
- **HIPAA**: US health information.
- **PCI DSS**: anyone handling payment cards (a contract standard, not a law).
- **SOX** (Sarbanes–Oxley): US public companies' financial records.
- **NIST** publishes the US government's security frameworks; **ISO 27001** is the international standard for a security program, which companies can be certified against.

## Data and privacy
Data is **classified** by sensitivity (public, internal, confidential, restricted). Roles: the **data owner** decides how it's used; the **custodian** looks after it day to day; the **controller** decides why personal data is processed, the **processor** handles it for them. A **data protection officer (DPO)** oversees privacy under GDPR.

People's rights under GDPR include access to their data and the **right to be forgotten** (erasure). **Data sovereignty** means data is subject to the laws of the country where it's stored.

> Often confused: a policy says *what*; a procedure says *how*.
:::
