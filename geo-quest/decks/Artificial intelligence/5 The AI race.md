## The race
related: China, United States
- Since 2018 commentators such as Ian Bremmer and Kai-Fu Lee have described a race between the US and China as an AI {{Cold War|what kind of war?}}.

- The worry that rivals racing for AI skip safety, as in past races for nuclear weapons, is called an AI {{arms race|what kind of race?}}.

## Hardware and money
- Training and running AI depends on graphics chips (GPUs), above all those designed by {{Nvidia|which company?}}.
- In July 2025 Nvidia became the first company worth {{$4 trillion|how much?}}; it passed $5 trillion that October.
- The US restricted exports of its most advanced AI {{chips|what?}} to China, to slow China's progress in AI and supercomputing.
- {{Stargate|what project?}}, announced in 2025 by OpenAI, SoftBank, Oracle and others with President Trump, plans about $500 billion of AI data centers in the US by 2029.
- Much of the boom is paid for with loans and venture capital while companies like OpenAI spend far more than they earn: hence fears of a {{bubble|what kind of economic risk?}}.

## Military AI
- {{Project Maven|which project?}}, started in 2017, uses machine learning to pick out people and objects in US military drone video.
- Weapons that find and attack targets without a person deciding are called lethal {{autonomous weapons|what?}}, nicknamed "slaughterbots".
- Since 2017 a US Department of Defense directive has required a human {{in the loop|where?}} before autonomous weapons take a life.
- In 2017 diplomats at the UN couldn't even agree on how to {{define|do what?}} lethal autonomous weapons; most major powers still oppose a ban.
