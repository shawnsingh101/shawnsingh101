## Output
- GDP by expenditure: GDP = {{C + I + G + (X − M)}} (consumption, investment, government spending, net exports).
- {{Real GDP|what?}} is GDP measured at constant prices, so it moves with output, not with inflation.
- Nominal GDP ÷ real GDP × 100 gives the {{GDP deflator|what?}}.

## Inflation
- Core CPI excludes {{food and energy}} prices.
- The Federal Reserve's inflation target is {{2%}}, measured by the {{PCE price index}}.
- High inflation together with weak growth and high unemployment is called {{stagflation|what?}}.
- The Fisher equation: real interest rate ≈ {{nominal interest rate − expected inflation}}.

## Monetary policy
- The Fed's dual mandate is maximum {{c1::employment|what?}} and stable {{c1::prices|what?}}.
- The rate the Fed targets is the {{federal funds rate}}, the overnight rate banks charge each other.
- The Taylor rule sets the policy rate from {{inflation's gap from target}} and {{the output gap}}.

## Bonds
- When short-term yields are above long-term yields, the yield curve is {{inverted|what?}}, often read as a recession warning.
- When interest rates rise, existing bond prices {{fall}}.
