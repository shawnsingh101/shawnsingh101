Exam-style practice on Lecture 1: reading real and nominal GDP data, average growth over many years, and inflation.

## Reading the data
- UK nominal GDP in 1996 was far below real GDP in 1996, because 1996 prices were {{lower|higher or lower?}} than in the base year.
- Average annual growth over $n$ years: $\left(\dfrac{GDP_n}{GDP_0}\right)^{ {{1/n}} } - 1$
eq: Average annual growth | \left(\frac{GDP_n}{GDP_0}\right)^{1/n} - 1
- UK real GDP rose from 1.24 to 1.87 trillion pounds between 1996 and 2016: about {{2%|what rate?}} a year.
- Food output grows 5% and software 20%, each half of base-year value: real GDP grows {{12.5%|how much?}}.
- In a year when firms sell more than they produce, the change in inventories is {{negative|positive or negative?}}.

## Inflation
- Nominal GDP grows 5% and inflation is 3%: real GDP grows about {{2%|how much?}}.
- The GDP deflator goes from 100 to 106.7: inflation was {{6.7%|how much?}}.
- Pay rises 4% while prices rise 6%: the real wage {{falls|rises or falls?}} by about 2%.
