## Problems
- $\pi^e = 3\%$, $h = 0.5$, $u = 7\%$, $\bar{u} = 5\%$: inflation is {{2\%|how much?}}.
eq: Expectations-augmented Phillips curve | \pi = \pi^e - h\,(u - \bar{u})
- Unemployment is 8% and the natural rate 5%: by Okun's law output is {{6\%|how much?}} below full employment.
eq: Okun's law | \frac{\bar{Y} - Y}{\bar{Y}} = 2\,(u - \bar{u})
- A central bank keeps unemployment below the natural rate year after year: inflation keeps {{rising|does what?}}, as expectations catch up.
- Inflation and unemployment both rise after an oil shock: that's a {{supply|which kind of?}} shock, not a move along the curve.
