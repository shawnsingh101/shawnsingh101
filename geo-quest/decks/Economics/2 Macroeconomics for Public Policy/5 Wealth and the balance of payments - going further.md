Exam-style practice on Lecture 5: recording transactions in the balance of payments, and moving between the national saving and current account identities.

## Recording transactions
- The US government pays off a 1 million dollar Treasury bill held by an Italian with dollar banknotes: US foreign liabilities in bills fall by 1 and in currency {{rise by 1|what happens?}}, so the balance is unchanged.
eq: Balance of payments | CA + KA = \Delta FA - \Delta FL
- It pays instead with a check on a US deposit in a Swiss bank: $\Delta FL = -1$ and $\Delta FA = $ {{-1|how much?}}.
eq: Balance of payments | CA + KA = \Delta FA - \Delta FL
- It pays instead by shipping 1 million dollars of corn: US exports rise by 1, so the current account rises by {{1|how much?}} and foreign debt falls by 1.
eq: Balance of payments | CA + KA = \Delta FA - \Delta FL
- Under the older BPM5 convention, $KA - \Delta FA + \Delta FL$ was called the capital and {{financial|what?}} account.

## The identities with numbers
- $Y = 1000$, $NI = -30$, $NUT = 10$: GNDI is {{980|how much?}}.
eq: Gross national disposable income | GNDI = Y + NI + NUT
- $I = 200$, $NX = -40$, $NI = -30$, $NUT = 10$: national saving is {{140|how much?}}.
eq: National saving | S = I + NX + NI + NUT
- In that economy the current account is {{-60|how much?}}.
eq: Current account | \Delta NFA = CA = NX + NI + NUT = S - I = GNDI - ABS
- With GNDI of 980 and a current account of $-60$, absorption is {{1040|how much?}}.
eq: Current account | \Delta NFA = CA = NX + NI + NUT = S - I = GNDI - ABS
- Fill in the missing step:
```derive
# Where national saving goes
S = Y + NI + NUT - (C + G) :: National income minus current spending.
S = ({{C + I + G + NX}}) + NI + NUT - (C + G) :: Replace GDP with the expenditure approach.
S = I + NX + NI + NUT :: C and G cancel.
```
eq: National saving | S = I + NX + NI + NUT

## What the balance of payments misses
- A fall in the dollar raises the dollar value of Americans' foreign assets without any {{transaction|what?}}, so it shows in the investment position but not the balance of payments.
- Counting all of $G$ as current spending overstates current spending, because $G$ includes public {{investment|what?}}.
- Counting wear and tear, a closed economy's wealth grows by saving minus the {{depreciation|what?}} of existing capital.
