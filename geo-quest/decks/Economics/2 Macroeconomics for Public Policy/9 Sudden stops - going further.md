## Debt dynamics
- A country owes 100 ($NFA_1 = -100$) at 5% and runs $NX = 20$ with $NUT = 5$: next period it owes {{80|how much?}}.
eq: Net foreign assets over time | NFA_2 = (1+r)\,NFA_1 + NX_1 + NUT_1
- $NFA_1 = -100$, $r = 10\%$, no transfers, $NX_1 = -10$: to stay solvent, $NX_2$ must be {{132|how much?}}.
eq: A country's intertemporal budget constraint | NX_1 + \frac{NX_2}{1+r} + NUT_1 + \frac{NUT_2}{1+r} + (1+r)\,NFA_1 = 0
- A country can run trade deficits forever in present value if it receives enough {{transfers|what?}} or starts with positive wealth.

## Pricing country risk
- World rate 4%, default probability 10%, recovery rate 0.5: the risky country pays about {{9.5%|what rate?}}.
eq: Country risk premium | 1 + r_H = \frac{1+r}{1 - p\,(1-x)}
- With a recovery rate of 1, the country's rate equals the {{world rate|what?}}: there's no risk.
eq: Country risk premium | 1 + r_H = \frac{1+r}{1 - p\,(1-x)}
- A country's bonds pay a fixed amount at maturity; if default looks likelier, their price today {{falls|rises or falls?}}, raising their yield.
- A higher country rate shrinks the deficit: saving rises because consumption {{falls|rises or falls?}}, not because income rose.

## Reversals
- When foreign assets in a country become more attractive, its interest rate falls and its current account deficit {{widens|widens or narrows?}}.
- A sudden stop raises the country's interest rate, so saving rises and investment {{falls|rises or falls?}}.
