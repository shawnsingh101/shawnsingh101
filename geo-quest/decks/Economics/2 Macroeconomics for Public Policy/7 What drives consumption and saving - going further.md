## Propensities
- A 100 dollar one-time bonus raises spending by 60: the marginal propensity to save is {{0.4|what number?}}.
eq: Marginal propensity to consume | MPC = \frac{\Delta C}{\Delta Y}, \qquad MPS = 1 - MPC
- With log utility and $\beta = 0.6$, the marginal propensity to consume out of current income is $\tfrac{1}{1+\beta} = $ {{0.625|what number?}}.
eq: Optimal consumption | C_1^* = \frac{1}{1+\beta}\left(Y_1 + \frac{Y_2}{1+r}\right)
- A government sends rebate checks: spending rises most among households that face a binding {{borrowing constraint|what?}}.

## Signing the effects
- Future income rises: $C_1$ rises, $C_2$ rises, and $S_1$ {{falls|rises or falls?}}.
- Wealth rises: its effect on saving matches a rise in {{future income|current or future income?}}.
- A lender facing a higher real rate: substitution says consume less today, income says consume {{more|more or less?}} today.
- A borrower facing a higher real rate consumes {{less|more or less?}} in both periods, since the income effect makes them poorer.
- A higher rate has no wealth effect on assets like bank deposits, whose {{future value|present or future value?}} adjusts instead.
- An aging population, by the life-cycle model, makes national saving {{fall|rise or fall?}}.
