A walk through the home of Congress: how it was built, then from the visitors' entrance up through the Crypt and the Rotunda to the chambers. Washington, D.C.

## The building
related: Washington, D.C., United States Capitol
- {{William Thornton|who?}}, a physician and amateur architect, won the 1793 competition to design the Capitol.
![William Thornton](commons:William-thorton.png)
- After the British burned the Capitol in 1814, it was rebuilt by {{Benjamin Henry Latrobe|who?}} and then Charles Bulfinch.
![Benjamin Henry Latrobe](commons:Benjamin latrobe by peale.jpg)
- The present dome, finished in 1866 during and after the Civil War, was designed by {{Thomas U. Walter|who?}}.
![Thomas U. Walter](commons:Thomas U. Walter - Brady-Handy.jpg)
- The dome is made of {{cast iron|what material?}}, painted to look like stone.
![The Capitol dome](commons:US Capitol Dome High Res Jan 2006.jpg)
- The bronze {{Statue of Freedom|which statue?}} stands on top of the dome.
![The Statue of Freedom](commons:Statue of Freedom, Washington, D.C.jpg)
- The Capitol stands at the east end of the {{National Mall|what?}}, facing the Washington Monument and the Lincoln Memorial.
![The Capitol and the Mall from the air](commons:Aerial of the U.S. Capitol under restoration, Washington, D.C LCCN2010630859.jpg)

## Inside
- Visitors enter underground through the {{Capitol Visitor Center|what?}}, opened in 2008; its great hall is Emancipation Hall.
![Emancipation Hall](commons:Emancipation-Hall 1.jpg)
- Under the Rotunda, the {{Crypt|which room?}}'s 40 columns hold up the floor above; it was meant for George Washington's tomb.
![The Crypt](commons:Us capitol crypt.jpg)
- The great round hall under the dome is the {{Rotunda|which room?}}, where presidents and other Americans have lain in state.
![The Rotunda](commons:USCapitolRotunda.JPG)
- The fresco in the eye of the dome, showing George Washington rising to the heavens, is {{The Apotheosis of Washington|which painting?}}.
![The Apotheosis of Washington](commons:Flickr - USCapitol - Apotheosis of Washington.jpg)
- The Apotheosis and much of the Capitol's painted decoration are by {{Constantino Brumidi|who?}}, an Italian-born artist.
![Constantino Brumidi](commons:Portrait photograph of Constantino Brumidi.jpg)
native: Costantino Brumidi (Italian)
- The old House chamber is now {{National Statuary Hall|which room?}}, where each state shows statues of notable people.
![National Statuary Hall](commons:Flickr - USCapitol - National Statuary Hall - U.S. Capitol.jpg)
- The {{Old Supreme Court Chamber|which room?}}, under the old Senate chamber, was the Supreme Court's home until 1860.
- The Senate meets in the north wing; the House of Representatives meets in the {{south|which wing?}}.
- The {{Senate chamber|which room?}} holds 100 desks, some of them made in 1819.
![The Senate chamber](commons:United States Senate Floor.jpg)
- The {{Hall of Columns|which hall?}}, on the House side, is lined with 28 marble columns topped with tobacco leaves and corn.
![The Hall of Columns](commons:HallofColumns.jpg)
