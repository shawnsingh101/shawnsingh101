A walk through the president's house: how it was built, then room by room from the ground floor up, then the West Wing where the work is done. Washington, D.C.

## The house
related: Washington, D.C., White House
- The Irish-born architect {{James Hoban|who?}} won the 1792 competition to design the President's House.
![James Hoban](commons:James Hoban circa 1800 - Crop.jpg)
- Hoban modeled the house in part on {{Leinster House|which building?}} in Dublin, now home of Ireland's parliament.
![The White House, south side](commons:WhiteHouseSouthFacade.JPG)
- In 1800 {{John Adams|which president?}} became the first president to live in the White House.
- In 1814, during the War of 1812, {{British troops|who?}} set the house on fire; only the outer walls survived.
![The British burning Washington, 1814](commons:British Burning Washington.jpg)
- The curved {{Truman Balcony|what?}} was added to the south side's portico in 1948, during a rebuilding that kept only the outer walls.
![The Truman Balcony](commons:Truman Balcony, 1987.jpg)
- The main building, where the president's family lives, is called the {{Executive Residence|what?}}.
![The Executive Residence](commons:White House 06.02.08.jpg)
- The {{East Wing|which wing?}}, home of the first lady's offices and the visitors' entrance, was torn down in 2025 to make way for a ballroom.
![The East Wing in 1992](commons:White House East Wing from SE in 1992.jpg)

## The state rooms
- Ambassadors and guests arrive through the oval {{Diplomatic Reception Room|which room?}} on the ground floor, where Franklin Roosevelt gave his fireside chats.
![President Reagan in the Diplomatic Reception Room](commons:President Ronald Reagan Greeting Walter Cronkite for An Interview in The Diplomatic Reception Room - DPLA - 5da0d9ca2f152bd4c7797c362c34143a.jpg)
- The largest room in the house, used for receptions, concerts and bill signings, is the {{East Room|which room?}}.
![The East Room](commons:A corner of the East Room, White House LCCN2006680305.jpg)
> Abraham Lincoln and John F. Kennedy lay in repose here.
- The oval {{Blue Room|which room?}}, in the middle of the state floor, is where the president receives guests at formal events.
- State dinners for visiting leaders are held in the {{State Dining Room|which room?}}.
![A state dinner for the president of France](commons:State Dinner - The Official State Visit of France (26832269817).jpg)
- On the second floor, the room Abraham Lincoln used as his office is now the {{Lincoln Bedroom|which room?}}.

## Where the work happens
- The president's staff work in the {{West Wing|which wing?}}, added in 1902 under Theodore Roosevelt.
![The West Wing](commons:Westwing commons.jpg)
- The president's own office, first built in 1909 under William Howard Taft, is the {{Oval Office|which room?}}.
![The Oval Office](commons:View of Oval Office in 2017.jpg)
- The desk in the Oval Office, made from the timbers of a British Arctic ship and given by Queen Victoria in 1880, is the {{Resolute desk|which desk?}}.
![Barack Obama at the Resolute desk](commons:Barack Obama sitting at the Resolute desk 2009.jpg)
- The president meets the heads of the departments in the {{Cabinet Room|which room?}}, beside the Oval Office.
- Crises are followed from the {{Situation Room|which room?}}, in the West Wing's basement.
![The Situation Room](commons:White House Situation Room Friday May 18 2007.jpg)
- The press secretary briefs reporters in the {{James S. Brady Press Briefing Room|which room?}}, named after Ronald Reagan's press secretary, shot in 1981.
![The press briefing room](commons:New Brady Briefing Room.jpg)
- Ceremonies and bill signings are held outside the Oval Office in the {{Rose Garden|which garden?}}.
![The Rose Garden](commons:Rosengarten des Weißen Hauses.jpg)
