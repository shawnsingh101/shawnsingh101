Animals people mix up, and the one thing you can see that tells them apart, no bones or teeth needed.

## Which whale?
- Baleen whales have no teeth: their mouths hold plates of {{baleen|what?}} that sieve food from the water.
![Baleen](commons:Baleen.jpg)
- Baleen whales have {{two|how many?}} blowholes; toothed whales have one.
- A whale with a huge, square, box-like head is a {{sperm whale|which whale?}}.
![A sperm whale and her calf](commons:Mother and baby sperm whale.jpg)
- A whale with very long white flippers and knobbly bumps on its head is a {{humpback whale|which whale?}}.
![Humpback whales](commons:Humpback whales in singing position.jpg)
- A big whale with no back fin, a smooth back and rough white patches on its head is a {{right whale|which whale?}}.
![A North Atlantic right whale and calf](commons:Eubalaena glacialis with calf.jpg)

## Which small toothed whale?
- A {{dolphin|which?}} has a beak and a curved, hooked back fin.
![A bottlenose dolphin](commons:Tursiops truncatus 01-cropped.jpg)
- A {{porpoise|which?}} has no beak, a rounded face and a small triangular back fin.
![A harbor porpoise](commons:Daan Close Up.PNG)

## Which flippered animal?
- A {{sea lion|which?}} has small ear flaps and walks on its big front flippers.
![A California sea lion](commons:Kalifornischer Seelöwe (Zalophus californianus), Territorialverhalten.jpg)
- A {{seal|which?}} has only ear holes, short front flippers, and wriggles on its belly on land.
![A harbor seal](commons:Common Seal Phoca vitulina.jpg)

## Which crocodilian?
- An {{alligator|which?}} has a broad U-shaped snout, and its lower teeth hide when its mouth is shut.
![An American alligator](commons:American Alligator.jpg)
- A {{crocodile|which?}} has a narrow V-shaped snout, and the big fourth lower tooth shows when its mouth is shut.
![A Nile crocodile](commons:Crocodylus niloticus6.jpg)

## Which elephant?
- The {{African elephant|which?}} has huge ears shaped like the map of Africa, and both sexes have tusks.
![An African bush elephant](commons:African Bush Elephant.jpg)
- The {{Asian elephant|which?}} has smaller rounded ears, a two-domed head, and only some males have tusks.
![An Asian elephant](commons:Elephas maximus (Bandipur).jpg)

## Which spotted cat?
- A {{cheetah|which cat?}} has solid round spots and black "tear lines" running from its eyes.
![A cheetah](commons:TheCheethcat.jpg)
- A {{leopard|which cat?}} has rosettes: rings of spots with plain centers.
![A leopard](commons:Male leopard - Mara.jpg)
- A {{jaguar|which cat?}} has bigger rosettes with spots inside them, and a heavier build.
![A jaguar](commons:Onça-pintada-PE Encontro das Águas-Thiagomarcelcampi(001).jpg)

## Which big black bird?
- A {{raven|which bird?}} is bigger, with a wedge-shaped tail, a heavy beak and shaggy throat feathers.
![A common raven](commons:Corvus corax ad berlin 090516.jpg)
- A {{crow|which bird?}} is smaller, with a fan-shaped tail and a smooth throat.
![A carrion crow](commons:Carrion crow 2022 04 05 05 02.jpg)

## Which long-eared animal?
- A {{hare|which?}} has longer, black-tipped ears and longer legs, and is born furred with its eyes open.
![A hare](commons:Hare Hare (20300967221).jpg)
- A {{rabbit|which?}} has shorter ears and legs, and is born naked and blind in a burrow.
![A European rabbit](commons:Oryctolagus cuniculus Tasmania 2 (cropped).jpg)
