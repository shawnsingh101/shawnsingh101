title: What bacteria are
---
The first of life's three domains: single cells with no nucleus. The oldest, most numerous and most varied living things.

## The simplest cells
- A bacterium is a single cell whose DNA floats free inside it, with no {{nucleus|what?}} around it.
- Bacteria multiply by splitting in two; in good conditions E. coli divides every {{20 minutes|how often?}}.
![Escherichia coli bacteria under an electron microscope](commons:EscherichiaColi NIAID.jpg)
- Your body holds about as many bacteria as {{cells of its own|what?}}: some 38 trillion, most of them in the gut.

## Bacteria that changed the world
- Cyanobacteria were the first living things to make oxygen by {{photosynthesis|how?}}; their oxygen filled the air about 2.4 billion years ago.
![Stromatolites in Shark Bay, Australia: mounds built by cyanobacteria](commons:Stromatolites in Sharkbay.jpg)
- The chloroplasts that make plants green descend from {{cyanobacteria|which bacteria?}} taken in by an early cell.
- Mitochondria, the power plants of our cells, were once free-living bacteria; they still carry their own {{DNA|what?}}.
- The bacterium Yersinia pestis caused the {{Black Death|which pandemic?}} of the 1340s.
- In 1928 {{Alexander Fleming|who?}} saw a mold kill the bacteria on a dish: the discovery of penicillin.
