## The animal
A **rorqual** (family Balaenopteridae), the baleen whales with pleated throats that gulp their food. Long and slender, mottled blue-gray (blue under water), with a tiny dorsal fin far back, a broad U-shaped head, and **70 to 400** black baleen plates in its upper jaw. Its closest living relatives, from its genome, are the **sei whale**, then the gray whale; it's not especially close to the fin whale, the second-largest, though the two sometimes **hybridize**.

## Size
Up to about **30 m** (98–100 ft) scientifically measured, and up to about **190 t** for the heaviest weighed, a female caught in the Southern Ocean in 1947. Females are bigger than males. Its heart, about **180 kg**, is the largest of any animal; a calf is born **6–7 m** long and about **2–3 t**, and gains about **90 kg a day** on its mother's milk.

## Feeding
Almost only **krill**, caught by **lunge feeding**: speeding up, opening the mouth up to 80°, engulfing up to about **220 t of water** in one gulp, then squeezing it out through the baleen with the throat pouch and tongue. It needs dense swarms to make it pay, and eats around **4 t of krill a day** in the feeding season. Each population summers near the poles to feed and winters toward the tropics to breed.

## Voice
Among the **loudest** (up to 188 dB) and **lowest** (8–25 Hz, mostly below human hearing) calls of any animal; songs differ from population to population, and in several they have been dropping in pitch for decades.

## Whaling and recovery
The new **harpoon cannon** (1860s) and **factory ships** (1920s) made blue whales catchable: about **360,000** were killed in the Antarctic alone in the first half of the 20th century, 30,000 in the 1930–31 season. The **International Whaling Commission** banned all blue whale hunting in **1966** (the Soviet Union hunted illegally into the 1970s). From at least 140,000 mature animals in 1926, there are perhaps **10,000–25,000** today. It is listed as **Endangered**; the main threats now are **ship strikes**, entanglement, ocean noise and climate change.
