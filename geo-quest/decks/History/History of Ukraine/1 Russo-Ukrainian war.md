The war Russia began against Ukraine in 2014, and its full-scale invasion of 24 February 2022: the largest and deadliest war in Europe since World War II. A live topic: every Wednesday the Wikipedia articles it follows are checked, and new cards or updates to the "as of" cards are suggested. Written from Wikipedia, November 2026.

## Before the invasion
related: Ukraine, Russia, Crimea
- In 1994 Ukraine gave up its {{nuclear weapons|what?}} in return for security assurances from Russia, the US and the UK: the Budapest Memorandum.
- The protests that began in late 2013, when President Yanukovych backed away from an agreement with the EU, are called {{Euromaidan|what?}}.
- In March {{2014|what year?}} Russia annexed Crimea, after soldiers with no insignia occupied it.
> The first time since World War II that a European country had annexed part of another.
- In April 2014 Russian-backed forces seized towns in the {{Donbas|which region?}}, proclaiming the Donetsk and Luhansk "people's republics".
- The {{Minsk agreements|what?}} of 2014 and 2015 tried to end the fighting in the Donbas; their ceasefires failed.

## The invasion
related: Kyiv, Belarus
- The full-scale invasion began on {{24 February 2022|when?}}.
- Putin called the invasion a {{"special military operation"|what did he call it?}}.
- Russia attacked on three fronts: from Belarus toward the capital, {{Kyiv|which city?}}; from Crimea in the south; and from the Donbas in the east.
- When Russian troops withdrew from around Kyiv in spring 2022, the killing of civilians in the town of {{Bucha|where?}} was uncovered.
- Russia captured the port city of {{Mariupol|which city?}} in 2022, after a destructive siege that ended at the Azovstal steelworks.

## Turning points
- In autumn 2022 Ukraine's counteroffensives retook most of {{Kharkiv|which province?}} Oblast, and in November the city of {{Kherson|which city?}}.
- In September 2022 Russia claimed to annex {{four|how many?}} Ukrainian provinces it only partly occupied.
- In May 2023, after one of the war's longest battles, Russia took the eastern city of {{Bakhmut|which city?}}.
- In August 2024 Ukraine launched its first offensive into Russia, into the {{Kursk|which region?}} region; by summer 2025 it had mostly been pushed back.
- By most reports, Russia had taken the eastern city of {{Pokrovsk|which city?}} by early 2026, after more than a year of fighting.
- Ukraine's biggest gains in two and a half years came in February 2026, when Russian forces were cut off from {{Starlink|which satellite internet?}}.
