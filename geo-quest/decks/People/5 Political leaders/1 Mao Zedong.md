## A life in brief

**Mao Zedong** (1893–1976) was born in **Shaoshan**, a village in **Hunan** province, to a prosperous peasant family,
in the last years of the Qing dynasty. He trained as a teacher in the provincial capital, **Changsha**, and in 1918
worked as an assistant in the library of **Peking University** under **Li Dazhao**, one of China's first Marxists.

### The Party
In 1921 Mao was one of the founding delegates of the **Chinese Communist Party** in Shanghai. Unlike Soviet orthodoxy,
which looked to factory workers, he came to see the **peasants** as the force of revolution. After the Nationalists
under **Chiang Kai-shek** turned on the Communists in 1927, he led a failed uprising and retreated to the
**Jinggang Mountains**, then built a Communist base in **Jiangxi**.

### The Long March
Encircled by Nationalist armies, the Communists broke out in 1934 on the **Long March**, a retreat of about 10,000 km
over a year that ended in **Shaanxi**, in the north. At the **Zunyi Conference** in January 1935 Mao rose to the
leadership. From **Yan'an** he fought the Japanese invasion in an uneasy alliance with the Nationalists, then won the
civil war.

### The People's Republic
On 1 October 1949 he proclaimed the **People's Republic of China** from the Gate of Heavenly Peace, **Tiananmen**.
Land reform and campaigns against "counter-revolutionaries" followed. The **Hundred Flowers** campaign (1956–57)
invited criticism, then punished those who spoke. The **Great Leap Forward** (1958–62), a drive to industrialize
through communes and backyard furnaces, caused a famine that killed an estimated **15 to 55 million** people.

### The Cultural Revolution
In 1966 Mao launched the **Cultural Revolution** to purge rivals and "old" ideas. Students formed the **Red Guards**,
waving the *Quotations from Chairman Mao*, the **Little Red Book**. Millions were persecuted; schools closed. In 1972 he
received **Richard Nixon** in Beijing. He died in 1976; his wife **Jiang Qing** and the rest of the **Gang of Four**
were arrested weeks later. In 1981 the Party judged him a great revolutionary whose later years were grave errors; his
embalmed body lies in a mausoleum on Tiananmen Square.
