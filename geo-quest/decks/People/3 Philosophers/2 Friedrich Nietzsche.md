## A life in brief

**Friedrich Nietzsche** (1844–1900) was born in **Röcken**, a village near Leipzig in Prussian Saxony, the son of a
Lutheran pastor who died when Friedrich was four. He went to the elite boarding school **Schulpforta**, then studied
theology and classical philology at Bonn and Leipzig, where he discovered **Schopenhauer**.

### Basel and Wagner
In 1869, at only **24** and before finishing his doctorate, he became professor of classical philology at the
**University of Basel**. He gave up his Prussian citizenship and stayed stateless for life. He became close to
**Richard Wagner**, and his first book, *The Birth of Tragedy* (1872), set the **Apollonian** (order) against the
**Dionysian** (frenzy) in Greek art. The friendship broke by 1878.

### The wanderer
Migraines, eye trouble and stomach illness forced him to resign in 1879. For ten years he lived on a small pension,
spending summers at **Sils Maria** in the Swiss Alps and winters in Nice, Genoa and Turin. On a walk by Lake Silvaplana
in 1881 came the idea of **eternal recurrence**. In 1882 he proposed to **Lou Salomé**, who turned him down. The books
came fast: *The Gay Science* ("**God is dead**"), ***Thus Spoke Zarathustra*** and the **Übermensch**, *Beyond Good and
Evil*, *On the Genealogy of Morals*, and the **will to power**.

### Turin and after
In January 1889 he collapsed in a street in **Turin**; the story is that he threw his arms around a horse being
whipped. He never recovered. His sister **Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche** took charge of him and his papers in **Weimar**,
edited them to suit her own nationalism and antisemitism (which he had despised), and built the cult that later let
the Nazis claim him. He died in 1900 and is buried in Röcken.
