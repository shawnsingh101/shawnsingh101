## A life in brief

**Immanuel Kant** (1724–1804) was born in **Königsberg**, the capital of East Prussia, today **Kaliningrad** in Russia.
His father was a harness maker; his mother raised him in **Pietism**, a strict, inward Lutheran faith. He studied at
the city's university, the **Albertina**, and then worked for years as a private tutor to country families.

### The teacher
He came back to lecture in 1755, paid by the students who came, on everything from logic and physics to geography.
The same year he proposed that the solar system formed from a spinning cloud of gas, an idea now called the
**Kant–Laplace nebular hypothesis**. In 1770 he finally became professor of logic and metaphysics.

### The silent decade
Reading **David Hume**, he said, woke him from his "dogmatic slumber." He published almost nothing for ten years, then,
at 57, the ***Critique of Pure Reason*** (1781): what we can know depends on the forms our own minds impose on
experience, a turn he compared to Copernicus. The *Critique of Practical Reason* (1788) and *Critique of Judgment*
(1790) followed, with the **categorical imperative**: act only on a rule you could will to be a universal law.

### The man
Kant never married, and hardly ever left Königsberg and its region. His daily routine was so exact that neighbors were
said to set their clocks by his afternoon walk; the story goes that he missed it only while reading Rousseau's *Emile*.
He was a witty host who liked long dinners with friends.

### Late writings and death
*What Is Enlightenment?* (1784) gave the motto **Sapere aude**, "dare to know." *Perpetual Peace* (1795) sketched a
league of free states to end war. He died in 1804; his last words were "Es ist gut" ("It is good"). He lies beside
Königsberg Cathedral, under his line about **the starry heavens above me and the moral law within me**.
