## A life in brief

**Kurt Gödel** (1906–1978) was born in **Brünn**, Austria-Hungary, today **Brno** in the Czech Republic, to a
German-speaking family; his father managed a textile mill. As a boy he asked so many questions his family called him
**Herr Warum**, "Mr. Why."

### Vienna
At the **University of Vienna** he switched from physics to mathematics and sat in on the **Vienna Circle** of
philosophers around Moritz Schlick. His doctorate (1929) proved the **completeness theorem** of logic. Then, in 1931,
at 25, he published the **incompleteness theorems**: any consistent system rich enough for arithmetic contains true
statements it cannot prove, and it cannot prove its own consistency. He first announced them at a conference in
**Königsberg**, Kant's city, in 1930; **John von Neumann** grasped them at once.

He married **Adele Nimbursky**, a dancer six years older whom he met at a Vienna nightclub; his family disapproved.

### Escape
After the Nazi annexation of Austria in 1938, Gödel was attacked in the street and called up for military service. In
1940 he and Adele crossed the Soviet Union on the **Trans-Siberian Railway**, sailed from Japan to San Francisco, and
went on to **Princeton**.

### Princeton
At the **Institute for Advanced Study** he became **Einstein**'s closest friend; Einstein said he came to his office
"just to have the privilege of walking home with Gödel." For Einstein's 70th birthday he found a solution to
Einstein's equations, the **rotating universe**, in which time travel is possible. At his 1947 **citizenship
hearing** he started telling the judge about a flaw in the US **Constitution** that could allow a dictatorship;
**Einstein** and **Oskar Morgenstern**, his witnesses, steered him back.

### The end
He grew more and more afraid of being poisoned and ate only food Adele prepared. When she was in hospital in 1977 he
stopped eating, and he died in January 1978, weighing about 65 pounds.
