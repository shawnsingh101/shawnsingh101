## A life in brief

**Demis Hassabis** (born 1976) grew up in north **London**, the son of a Greek Cypriot father and a Chinese Singaporean
mother. He was a **chess** prodigy, reaching master level at 13 and ranking second in the world for his age; prize
money bought him his first computer, a ZX Spectrum.

### Games
He finished school two years early, and at 17 co-designed and programmed the hit simulation game ***Theme Park***
(1994) at **Bullfrog Productions** with Peter Molyneux. He then read computer science at **Cambridge** (Queens'
College), worked on the AI of *Black & White* at Lionhead, and in 1998 founded his own studio, **Elixir Studios**. He
also won the Pentamind, the all-round games title of the Mind Sports Olympiad, five times.

### The brain
To learn how intelligence works he took a PhD in cognitive neuroscience at **University College London** (2009). He
showed that patients with damage to the **hippocampus**, the brain's memory center, also struggle to **imagine** new
scenes: memory and imagination share machinery. *Science* listed it among the breakthroughs of 2007.

### DeepMind
In 2010 he co-founded **DeepMind** with Shane Legg and Mustafa Suleyman, aiming to "solve intelligence." **Google**
bought it in 2014. Its programs taught themselves Atari games; in March 2016 **AlphaGo** beat **Lee Sedol**, one of the
world's best Go players, 4–1 in Seoul. In 2020 **AlphaFold** solved the 50-year-old problem of predicting a protein's
3D shape from its sequence, and DeepMind released predictions for some 200 million proteins.

### The Nobel
In 2024 Hassabis and **John Jumper** shared half the **Nobel Prize in Chemistry** for AlphaFold (David Baker got the
other half), and he was knighted. He leads **Google DeepMind**, which makes the Gemini models, and the drug-discovery
company **Isomorphic Labs**.
