## A life in brief

**Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart** (1756–1791) was born in **Salzburg**, then a small state of the Holy Roman Empire ruled by its
prince-archbishop. His father **Leopold** was a court violinist and teacher who saw at once what his son could do.

### The wonder child
Wolfgang was composing at five. From 1762, Leopold took him and his gifted older sister **Nannerl** on tours that lasted
years: Munich, Vienna, Paris, and London, where the boy met Johann Christian Bach. In Italy, aged 14, he heard Allegri's
secret *Miserere* in the Sistine Chapel and wrote it out from memory.

### Salzburg and the break
Back home he worked for the new archbishop, **Colloredo**, who treated him as a servant. In 1781 Mozart quit, was
literally kicked out of the door by the archbishop's steward, and stayed in **Vienna** to live by teaching, concerts and
commissions: one of the first great composers to go freelance.

### Vienna
He married **Constanze Weber** in 1782, against his father's wishes. The Vienna decade brought the piano concertos,
the late symphonies and the great operas, three of them with the librettist **Lorenzo Da Ponte**: *The Marriage of
Figaro*, *Don Giovanni* and *Così fan tutte*. **Joseph Haydn** told Leopold his son was the greatest composer he knew.

### The last year
In 1791 came *The Magic Flute* and a mysterious, anonymous commission for a **Requiem**. Mozart fell ill and died that
December, the Requiem unfinished; his pupil **Süssmayr** completed it. He was buried in a common grave, as was usual in
Vienna then, at St. Marx. The legend that Salieri poisoned him is a legend; it lives on in the play and film *Amadeus*.
