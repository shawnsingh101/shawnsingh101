## A life in brief

**Frédéric Chopin** (1810–1849) was born in **Żelazowa Wola**, a village west of Warsaw, to a French father and a
Polish mother. He grew up in Warsaw, played in its salons as a child, and studied with **Józef Elsner** at the
Warsaw Conservatory.

### Leaving Poland
In November 1830 he left for Vienna; weeks later the **November Uprising** against Russian rule broke out, and its
defeat meant he never returned. He settled in **Paris** in 1831.

### Paris
Chopin disliked big concert halls and gave only about thirty public concerts in his life. He played in **salons**,
taught wealthy pupils, and wrote almost only for **solo piano**: nocturnes, études, ballades, preludes, and the Polish
dances, mazurkas and polonaises, that he made into concert music. **Liszt** was a friend.

### George Sand
In 1836 he met the novelist **George Sand** (Aurore Dupin). In the winter of 1838–39 they took her children to
**Majorca** for the warm air, and ended up in the cells of an empty Carthusian monastery at **Valldemossa**. It rained;
the locals shunned them; Chopin, probably already ill with **tuberculosis**, grew very sick; his Pleyel piano was held
at customs for weeks. There he finished the **24 Preludes**. Sand wrote about it in *A Winter in Majorca*. For years
after, summers were spent at her house at **Nohant**, where much of his best music was written. They parted in 1847.

### The end
In 1848 his Scottish pupil **Jane Stirling** took him on a tour of Britain; he gave his last concert in London. He died
in Paris in October 1849, aged 39. Mozart's Requiem was sung at his funeral; he lies in **Père Lachaise**. His sister
carried his **heart** to Warsaw, where it rests in a pillar of the Holy Cross Church.
