## A life in brief

**Vincent van Gogh** (1853–1890) was born in **Zundert**, in the south of the Netherlands, the son of a Protestant
pastor. At 16 he joined the art dealers **Goupil & Cie**, working in The Hague, London and Paris, until he was
dismissed. He tried teaching and then preaching, living among coal miners in the **Borinage** in Belgium. At 27 he
decided to become an artist, largely self-taught.

### Dark beginnings
At his parents' home in **Nuenen** he painted peasants and weavers in earthy browns, and in 1885 his first major
work, ***The Potato Eaters***.

### Paris
In 1886 he moved in with his brother **Theo**, an art dealer who supported him all his life. He met the
Impressionists and pointillists, collected Japanese woodblock prints, and his color turned bright.

### Arles
In February 1888 he went south to **Arles** in Provence, dreaming of a colony of artists. He rented the **Yellow
House**, painted the **Sunflowers** to decorate it, and in October welcomed **Paul Gauguin**. Nine weeks of
quarrels ended on 23 December, when Vincent cut off part of his left ear.

### Saint-Rémy and Auvers
In May 1889 he admitted himself to the asylum of **Saint-Paul-de-Mausole** at Saint-Rémy, where he painted *The
Starry Night* from the view outside his window. In May 1890 he moved to **Auvers-sur-Oise**, near Paris, under the
care of **Dr. Gachet**, and painted about a picture a day. On 27 July he shot himself in the chest and died two days
later, aged 37. Theo died six months after him.

### After
He sold very little in his life. Theo's widow, **Johanna van Gogh-Bonger**, kept the paintings and the hundreds of
letters and made him famous. Most of them are now at the **Van Gogh Museum** in Amsterdam.
