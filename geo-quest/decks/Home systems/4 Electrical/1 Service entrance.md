Where power comes into the house: the overhead drop or underground lateral, the meter, the 120/240-volt split, and the grounding electrode system that ties it all to the earth.

:::lesson
## From the utility to the outlet
```stack
# Power, in order
6 | Outlet or light | the device
5 | Branch circuit | cable from one breaker
4 | Main panel | main breaker, then a breaker per circuit
3 | Meter | measures kilowatt-hours
2 | Service drop or lateral | overhead or underground
1 | Utility transformer | on a pole or a pad
```
The utility's transformer turns the street's high voltage down to what a house uses. In North America that is **split-phase** power: two **hot** wires and a **neutral**. Each hot is 120 volts to the neutral, for lights and plugs; the two hots together give 240 volts, for big loads like a dryer, range, water heater or air conditioner.
:::

> The wires loop down before they go in (a drip loop), so rain runs off instead of following them inside.

## Volts and amps at the house
- A North American house gets two hot wires and a neutral: 120 volts from either hot to the neutral, and {{240 volts|how much?}} between the two hots.
- Big appliances like the dryer, range, water heater and central air run on {{240 volts|how much?}}.
- The most common service size in newer houses is {{200 amps|how much?}}; older houses often have 100 amps or less.

## The meter
- The {{electric meter|what?}} measures the energy the house uses, in kilowatt-hours.
![An electric meter](commons:Yipwm 1b (7691592010).jpg)
- A {{smart meter|what?}} sends its readings to the utility on its own, over a radio or power-line network, often every 15 minutes to an hour.
![A smart meter](commons:Itron OpenWay Electricity Meter with Two-Way Communications.JPG)

## Grounding
- The {{grounding electrode|what?}} system connects the electrical system to the earth, usually with metal rods driven into the ground and a clamp on the water pipe.
- A ground rod is usually a copper-clad steel rod at least {{8 feet|how long?}} long.
![A ground rod and its clamp](commons:HomeEarthRodAustralia1.jpg)
- Grounding gives a fault, or a lightning strike or utility surge, a safe path to the {{earth|where?}} instead of through a person.
