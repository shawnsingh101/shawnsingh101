The main panel and what's in it: the main breaker, branch breakers and fuses, subpanels, and how a breaker's size matches its wire.

## The panel
- The {{main panel|what?}} (service panel, breaker box or load center) splits the incoming power into the house's circuits.
![A residential breaker panel](commons:OpenClosedPanelboardEEUU(GE)CloseupTop.jpg)
- The {{main breaker|which breaker?}}, usually at the top of the panel, shuts off power to the whole house.
- A {{subpanel|what?}} is a smaller panel fed from the main one, for a garage, addition or basement.
![A subpanel](commons:Electrical panel and subpanel with cover removed from subpanel.jpg)
- In a subpanel, the neutral and ground wires are kept {{separate|together or separate?}}; they are joined only in the main panel.

## Overcurrent protection
- A {{circuit breaker|what?}} is a switch that trips off by itself when a circuit draws too much current; flipping it back resets it.
![A circuit breaker](commons:Jtecul.jpg)
- A single-pole breaker feeds a 120-volt circuit; a {{double-pole|which kind?}} breaker takes two spaces and feeds a 240-volt circuit.
- In older houses, screw-in {{fuses|what?}} protect circuits: a metal strip inside melts when the current is too high, and the fuse is replaced.
- Never replace a fuse with a {{larger|larger or smaller?}} one than the circuit's wire is rated for: the wire could overheat before the fuse blows.

## What trips a breaker
- Too many things running on one circuit at once is an {{overload|what?}}.
- A hot wire touching a neutral wire is a {{short circuit|what?}}.
- A hot wire touching a ground wire, a metal box or a person is a {{ground fault|what?}}.

## Breaker and wire sizes
- A breaker is sized to protect the circuit's {{wiring|what?}}: it must trip before the wires get hot enough to start a fire.
- A 15-amp circuit uses 14-gauge copper wire; a 20-amp circuit uses {{12-gauge|what size?}}.
- A 30-amp circuit, like an electric dryer's, uses {{10-gauge|what size?}} copper wire.
> In wire gauge (AWG), the smaller the number, the thicker the wire.
