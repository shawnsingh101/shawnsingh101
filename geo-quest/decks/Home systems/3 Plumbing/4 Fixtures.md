## What a fixture is
In plumbing, a **fixture** is anything fixed in place that uses water and drains it: sinks, tubs, showers, toilets, the washing-machine box. Every fixture has a supply (hot, cold or both, each with its own shutoff valve), a drain, and a trap.

## Inside a toilet
![Inside a toilet (drawn for this app)](figures/home/toilet.svg)
Pressing the handle pulls a chain that lifts the **flapper**. About a gallon and a half of water rushes out of the tank into the bowl, through the holes under the rim and a jet at the bottom. The bowl fills faster than it can drain, so water spills over the hump in the **trapway** and fills it: that starts a **siphon**, which pulls the bowl empty with the familiar gurgle. When air gets into the trapway the siphon breaks.

Meanwhile the flapper has dropped shut and the **fill valve** opens because its float has fallen. It refills the tank, and through the **refill tube** it also tops up the bowl, so the trapway is full of water again to block sewer gas. When the float rises to the set level, the fill valve shuts.

## Sink and tub stoppers
![A sink's pop-up drain (drawn for this app)](figures/home/pop-up-drain.svg)
A bathroom sink's pop-up stopper is moved by a lever under the sink: the **lift rod** behind the faucet joins a strap with holes (the **clevis**), which joins the **pivot rod**. The pivot rod passes through a ball-shaped seal into the drain pipe and under the stopper, so it works like a seesaw. Tubs have either a **plunger** drain (a plug moves up and down inside the overflow pipe, so there's no stopper in the drain) or a **pop-up** drain (a stopper in the drain, rocked by a linkage).

## Four kinds of faucets
| Kind | How it controls the water | What usually wears out |
|---|---|---|
| Compression | two handles screw a rubber washer down onto a seat | the washer |
| Ball | one handle rotates a slotted ball over the hot and cold inlets | the two rubber seats and springs |
| Cartridge | one handle slides and turns a tube with holes | the O-rings or the cartridge |
| Disk | one handle slides one ceramic disk over another | the rubber seals under the disks |

Compression faucets are the oldest kind and always have two handles. The other three are "washerless", usually with one handle: up and down sets the flow, side to side the temperature.
![A compression valve (drawn for this app)](figures/home/compression-faucet.svg)

## Showers and outdoor faucets
A tub-and-shower control is a faucet with one more part, the **diverter**, which sends water to the spout or the shower head. Many showers also have an **anti-scald valve** that holds the temperature steady when someone flushes a toilet. An outdoor faucet in a cold climate should be **frost-free**: its valve sits at the end of a long stem, inside the warm house, so the part in the cold wall drains empty after you shut it off.

## Inside a toilet
![Inside a toilet (drawn for this app)](figures/home/toilet.svg)
- Pressing the flush handle pulls a chain that lifts the {{flapper|which part?}} off the hole at the bottom of the tank.
- The {{fill valve|which part?}} (ballcock) lets water into the tank until the float rises and shuts it off.
- While the tank refills, the {{refill tube|which part?}} sends a little water down the overflow tube to refill the bowl.
- If the fill valve fails to shut off, the water spills into the {{overflow tube|which part?}} instead of onto the floor.
- A toilet's trap is hidden: it's the {{trapway|what?}}, an S-shaped channel cast into the base.
- A toilet flushes by {{siphon|what action?}}: water rushing in fills the trapway, which then pulls the bowl empty.
- The toilet seals to the drain pipe with a {{wax ring|what?}} pressed onto the closet flange in the floor.
- Since 1994, a new toilet in the US may use no more than {{1.6 gallons|how much?}} per flush.
> Older toilets often use 3.5 gallons or more; WaterSense-labeled ones use 1.28 or less.

## Sink and tub stoppers
- On a bathroom sink, pulling up the {{lift rod|which part?}} behind the faucet closes the stopper.
- Under the sink, the lift rod moves the {{pivot rod|which part?}}, which goes through the side of the drain pipe and lifts or lowers the stopper.
![A sink's pop-up drain (drawn for this app)](figures/home/pop-up-drain.svg)
- The strap with a row of holes that joins the lift rod to the pivot rod is the {{clevis|what?}}.
- A tub with a lever on its overflow plate but no stopper in the drain has a {{plunger|which kind?}} drain: the lever lowers a plug inside the overflow pipe.
- A tub drain with a stopper in the opening, rocked up and down by a linkage from the overflow lever, is a {{pop-up|which kind?}} drain.

## Faucets
- A faucet with separate hot and cold handles that screw a rubber washer down onto a seat is a {{compression|which kind?}} faucet.
![A compression valve (drawn for this app)](figures/home/compression-faucet.svg)
- A single-handle faucet with a slotted hollow sphere inside is a {{ball|which kind?}} faucet.
- A faucet whose one moving part is a tube with holes that slides up and down and turns is a {{cartridge|which kind?}} faucet.
- A disk faucet controls the water with two polished {{ceramic|what material?}} disks sliding over each other; they rarely wear out.
- On a single-handle faucet, lifting the handle sets the flow and turning it side to side sets the {{temperature|what?}}.
- A dripping compression faucet usually needs a new {{washer|what part?}}.
- A leak from under a compression faucet's handle is stopped by tightening the {{packing nut|which part?}}.

## Showers
- A tub-and-shower control's {{diverter|which valve?}} sends the water to either the tub spout or the shower head.
- An {{anti-scald|which kind?}} shower valve keeps the water from turning suddenly hot when someone flushes a toilet nearby.
> Two kinds: pressure-balancing (it reacts to a drop in cold-water pressure) and thermostatic (it reacts to the temperature itself).

## Outdoor faucets
- An outdoor faucet is a {{hose bibb|what?}}, or sillcock.
- A {{frost-free|which kind?}} sillcock has a long stem, so its valve shuts inside the warm house and the pipe in the cold wall drains empty.
- A frost-free sillcock can still freeze and burst if a {{hose|what?}} is left attached in freezing weather, since the water can't drain out.
- An outdoor faucet's {{vacuum breaker|what?}} keeps dirty water in a hose from being sucked back into the drinking water.
