The water heater: tank and tankless heaters, the parts inside a tank, and the two safety parts every system needs, the T&P relief valve and the expansion tank.

## Two kinds
- A {{tank water heater|which kind?}} keeps a store of water, often 40 to 50 gallons, hot all the time.
![A tank water heater](commons:Water Heater White.jpg)
- A {{tankless water heater|which kind?}} heats water only as it flows through, so it never runs out but has a limit on how much it can heat at once.
![A tankless water heater](commons:Warmwatergeiser.jpg)
- A {{heat pump water heater|which kind?}} moves heat from the surrounding air into the tank, using a fraction of the electricity of plain heating elements.

## Inside the tank
- Cold water enters a tank heater through the {{dip tube|which part?}}, which carries it to the bottom.
- The {{anode rod|which part?}}, magnesium or aluminum, corrodes away inside the tank so the steel tank doesn't.
> It is called a sacrificial anode; replacing it before it's used up makes the tank last longer.
- Minerals that settle at the bottom of the tank are {{sediment|what?}}, flushed out through the drain valve.
- A gas water heater sends its exhaust up a {{flue|what?}} through the middle of the tank and out a vent.
- To limit scalding, set the water heater to about {{120 °F|what temperature?}}.

## Safety parts
- The {{temperature and pressure relief valve|which valve?}} (T&P valve) opens if the tank gets too hot or the pressure too high, so it can't explode.
- A T&P relief valve is usually set to open at 150 psi or {{210 °F|what temperature?}}.
- The pipe from the T&P valve, the {{discharge pipe|what?}}, runs down to within a few inches of the floor and must never be capped.
- An {{expansion tank|what?}} on the cold line takes up the extra volume when water heats and expands, where a check valve or pressure-reducing valve keeps it from flowing back to the main.
![An expansion tank above a water heater](commons:Expansion tank.jpg)
