## A gas tank heater
![Inside a gas water heater (drawn for this app)](figures/home/gas-water-heater.svg)
Cold water enters through the **dip tube**, which carries it to the bottom, and hot water leaves from the top. A **gas control valve** on the front holds the thermostat: when the water cools below the setting, it opens the gas to the **burner**, which is lit by a small **pilot** flame (or an electronic igniter). A **thermocouple** sits in the pilot flame; if the pilot goes out, it cools and the valve shuts off the gas. The burner's exhaust rises through the **flue** in the middle of the tank, where a twisted metal **baffle** slows it so more heat goes into the water, then out through the **draft hood** into the vent.

## An electric tank heater
An electric heater has two **elements**, one high and one low, each with its own thermostat, and they take turns. The upper one heats the top of the tank first, so hot water is ready soon; when the top is hot, its thermostat switches the power to the lower element. Above the upper thermostat is a red **high-limit reset** button that cuts the power if the water gets dangerously hot.
- **No hot water at all**: press the reset button; check the two breakers; then the upper element or thermostat.
- **Some hot water, but it runs out fast**: the lower element or thermostat.

## Tankless and the boiler
A tank loses heat all day and night just keeping water hot (**standby loss**). A **tankless** heater keeps no store: a **flow sensor** feels water moving and fires a gas burner or electric elements, and a computer sets the heat to the outgoing temperature. Its limit is **flow**: too many taps at once and the water cools. A house heated by a **boiler** can heat its water in a coil inside the boiler (a **tankless coil**), or better, in a separate well-insulated **indirect** tank that boiler water heats through a coil, so the boiler fires only a few times a day in summer.

## Heat pump water heaters
A heat pump water heater works like a refrigerator run backwards into the tank: a fan pulls room air across a cold coil, the refrigerant carries that heat to a hot coil wrapped around the tank, and the room is left cooler and drier. It uses about a third to a half of the electricity of plain elements, which it keeps for backup.

## Tankless heaters
- A tank heater keeps losing heat around the clock just to keep water hot: its {{standby loss|what?}}.
- A tankless heater fires when its {{flow sensor|what?}} feels water moving through it.
![Inside an electric tankless water heater](commons:Water heater bathroom.JPG)
- A tankless heater's limit is its {{flow rate|what?}} in gallons per minute: open too many taps and the water cools.
- A gas tankless heater burns a lot of gas at once, so it often needs a {{larger|larger or smaller?}} gas line than a tank heater.

## Heated by the boiler
- A boiler can heat the house's water in a {{tankless coil|what?}} inside the boiler itself.
- An {{indirect water heater|what?}} is a separate, well-insulated tank heated by boiler water flowing through a coil inside it.
- A {{tempering valve|which valve?}} blends cold water into very hot water from a boiler coil, so the taps get a safe temperature.
