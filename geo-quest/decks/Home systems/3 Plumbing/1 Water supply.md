title: Water supply

How clean water gets into the house and out of the tap: from the city main or a well, through the meter and the main shutoff, along copper, PEX or CPVC pipe, to each fixture's own shutoff.

:::lesson
## Three systems of pipes
A house holds three separate sets of pipes. **Supply** pipes bring clean water in under pressure, so they can be small and run any direction. **Drain** pipes carry used water out by gravity, so they are bigger and always slope downhill. **Vent** pipes carry only air: they open through the roof and let air into the drains so water flows smoothly and the traps keep their water.
![A house's supply, drain and vent pipes (drawn for this app)](figures/home/three-systems.svg)

## From the street to the tap
```stack
# City water, in order
6 | Faucet | the fixture
5 | Fixture shutoff | under the sink or behind the toilet
4 | Supply pipes | copper, PEX or CPVC, hot and cold
3 | Main shutoff | stops water to the whole house
2 | Water meter | measures what you use
1 | Water main | under the street, through a curb stop
```
Supply water is under **pressure** (usually about 40 to 80 psi), so it can run uphill and around corners. Drains are the opposite: they work by gravity (see Drains and vents).

## On a well
A **submersible pump** down in the well pushes water up to a **pressure tank**: a tank with an air bladder that keeps the water under pressure, so the pump doesn't start every time someone opens a tap. A **pressure switch** turns the pump on when the pressure drops (often at 40 psi) and off when it's back up (often 60 psi).
:::

## On a well
- On a private well, a {{submersible pump|which pump?}} down in the well pushes water up to the house.
- A {{pressure tank|what?}} keeps well water under pressure, so the pump doesn't start every time a tap opens.
![A well pressure tank and its pressure switch](commons:Pressure tank and square D.jpg)
- On a well system, the {{pressure switch|what?}} turns the pump on when pressure falls and off when it's back up.

## Supply pipes
- {{Copper|which pipe?}} supply pipe is rigid, joined by soldering, and lasts for decades.
![Copper pipe](commons:Propane torch soldering copper pipe.jpg)
- {{PEX (cross-linked polyethylene)|which pipe?}} is flexible plastic tubing, run in long lengths with few joints; often red for hot and blue for cold.
- {{CPVC (chlorinated polyvinyl chloride)|which pipe?}} is rigid cream-colored plastic pipe, joined with solvent cement.
> Unlike ordinary white PVC, it handles hot water, so it can carry both hot and cold.
- Old {{galvanized steel|which pipe?}} supply pipe, common before the 1960s, rusts inside until the water flow slows to a trickle.
