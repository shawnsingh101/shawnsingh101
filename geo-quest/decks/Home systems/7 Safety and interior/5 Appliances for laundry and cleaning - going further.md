## Washers
A **top-loader** turns its clothes around a vertical axis: an **agitator** post (or a low **impeller** plate in high-efficiency models) churns them through the water, and then the whole basket spins to throw the water out. A **lid switch** stops the spin when the lid is opened.

A **front-loader** turns its drum on a horizontal axis. As the drum turns, paddles lift the clothes and gravity drops them back through a small pool of water, so it needs no agitator and uses much less water. It spins much faster, often over 1,000 rpm, so clothes come out drier and spend less time in the dryer, but that takes heavy counterweights and a solid floor. A front-loader needs **HE** (high-efficiency) detergent, in small amounts, and its rubber **door gasket** traps water: wipe it and leave the door ajar to stop mold and smells. A **drain pump filter** behind a small door at the bottom front catches coins and lint; clean it now and then (with a towel and a shallow pan ready).

Both kinds have small **inlet screens** where the hoses attach, which clog with grit and minerals. A machine that bangs and "walks" in the spin is unbalanced: redistribute the load, and adjust the **leveling feet** so all four sit firmly on the floor.

## Dryers
A dryer tumbles the clothes in a drum turned by a **belt** from the motor while a fan pulls heated air through them and out the **vent**. An **electric** dryer heats the air with a coil **element** on a 240-volt circuit; a **gas** dryer with a burner, lit by a **glow-bar igniter**, with a **flame sensor** that closes the gas valve if it doesn't light. Thermostats cycle the heat; a one-time **thermal fuse** cuts it if the dryer overheats, usually because the vent is clogged with lint. The vent duct should be rigid or semi-rigid metal, as short as possible: the code allows up to 35 feet, less 5 feet for each 90° bend.

## Coffee makers
A **percolator** boils water at the bottom of the pot, which pushes it up a central tube to spill over the grounds in a basket at the top and trickle back down, again and again. A **French press** steeps the grounds in hot water for a few minutes, then a mesh plunger presses them to the bottom. A drip machine heats water in a tube and lets it rise and drip once through the grounds.

## Vacuum cleaners
A vacuum's motor drives a fan that pulls air in fast enough to carry dirt with it; on carpet, a spinning **brush roll** (beater bar) shakes the dirt loose. The dirt then has to come out of the air before it's blown back into the room, by one of two methods: **filtering**, through a bag or filter whose fineness is a trade-off between catching fine dust and keeping the suction strong, or **cyclonic** separation, which spins the air so dirt is flung to the outside of the canister and falls into the bin. A **shop vacuum** relies mostly on the air slowing down in its big drum. A **central vacuum** has its motor and canister in the basement or garage, piping in the walls to **inlets** in each room, and blows its exhaust outdoors.

## Before calling for help
- **Won't start**: the breaker, the plug, and the door or lid switch (with a meter, a good switch reads zero ohms when pressed).
- **Fills slowly**: make sure the valves are open, then clean the inlet screens (soak minerals off in vinegar).
- **Drains slowly or leaves water**: clean the front-loader's drain pump filter; check the drain hose for a kink.
- **Suds on the floor**: too much detergent, or the wrong kind.
- **Water on the floor**: a loose or leaking hose, a kinked drain hose, a clogged house drain, or a dirty door seal.
- **A dryer takes too long**: clean the lint screen and the whole vent duct.
- **Gas dryer tumbles but won't heat**: out of gas (propane), or a failed igniter, gas valve coil or thermal fuse. **Won't tumble**: a broken belt, which is a simple repair.
- **A vacuum loses suction**: empty the bag or bin, clean the filters, and pull clogs out of the hose (don't push them further in). **The brush roll stopped**: replace its belt.
