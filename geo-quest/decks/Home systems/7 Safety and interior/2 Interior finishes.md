title: Interior finishes

The surfaces you see and touch: drywall and plaster walls, trim, cabinets and countertops, and the floor.

## Walls and ceilings
- Most interior walls today are {{drywall|what?}}: panels of gypsum between sheets of paper, screwed to the studs.
![Hanging drywall](commons:Drywall and tools.jpg)
- The seams between sheets of drywall are covered with tape and {{joint compound|what?}} (mud), then sanded smooth.
- Most walls use ½-inch drywall; garages and furnace rooms use fire-rated {{Type X|which type?}}, usually ⅝ inch thick.
- Before drywall, walls were troweled {{plaster|what?}} over strips of wood lath.
![Wood lath behind plaster](commons:Lathwork and plaster.jpg)

## Trim
- {{Baseboard|which trim?}} covers the joint where the wall meets the floor.
- The trim around a door or window opening is its {{casing|which trim?}}.
- {{Crown molding|which trim?}} covers the joint where the wall meets the ceiling.
![Crown molding](commons:Crown molding at Bray House, Kittery Point, Maine.jpg)

## Cabinets and counters
- {{Kitchen cabinets|what?}} are boxes, usually of plywood or particleboard, hung on the wall or set on the floor under the counters.
![Kitchen cabinets](commons:Newly renovated kitchen with cabinets refrigerator stove and hardwood floor.jpg)
- Engineered stone countertops, made of ground stone bound with resin, are called {{quartz|what?}}.
- A natural stone countertop that has to be sealed now and then is usually {{granite|which stone?}}.
![A granite countertop](commons:Kitchen stone countertops.jpg)

## Floors
- {{Solid hardwood|which flooring?}} is one piece of wood through; it can be sanded and refinished several times.
![Hardwood flooring](commons:Flooring.jpg)
- {{Engineered wood|which flooring?}} flooring has a thin layer of real wood on top of plywood.
- {{Laminate|which flooring?}} flooring is a photograph of wood under a clear wear layer, on fiberboard.
![Laminate flooring](commons:Laminate floor assembly - setting tongue and groove.JPG)
- {{Luxury vinyl plank|which flooring?}} (LVP) is waterproof plastic flooring made to look like wood.
- {{Ceramic or porcelain tile|which flooring?}} is set in mortar and joined with grout.
