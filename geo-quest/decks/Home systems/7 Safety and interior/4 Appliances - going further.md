## Why repair it yourself
A service call costs so much mostly because the repairer has to travel to the appliance. Many failures are simple: a tripped breaker, a clogged filter, a worn belt, a burned-out element that just plugs in. Keep each appliance's manual (or download it by model number): the model number unlocks parts diagrams, error codes and repair videos online.

## The dishwasher
A **fill valve** lets water in for a set time, and a **float switch** shuts it off if the level gets too high. An **element** in the bottom heats the water and later helps dry the dishes. A pump driven by the motor sends the water up to the **spray arms**, which spin on the push of their own jets. Many dishwashers use one pump for both jobs, with a valve that sends water to the arms during washing and to the drain at the end. The drain hose loops up under the counter or goes through an **air gap** on the sink, so sink water can't siphon back in. Newer models have a **filter** in the bottom that must be cleaned by hand.

## Electric and gas ranges
Each electric coil element is a wire inside a metal sheath; on most ranges it simply **plugs in**, and an **infinite switch** on the knob sets the heat by switching it on and off. A tester tells a good element from a bad one: a good one shows some resistance (tens of ohms) end to end; a burned-out one shows none getting through (an **open** circuit). The oven has a **bake** element at the bottom and a **broil** element at the top, and a **sensor** that tells the control how hot it is.

A gas burner mixes gas with air before it burns; an **air shutter** sets the mix. The flame should be crisp and **blue**; a lazy yellow flame means it needs more air or has clogged ports. Burners are lit by a spark **igniter** (the clicking), and can be lit with a match in a power outage. Most gas ovens use a **glow-bar** igniter that must get hot enough before the gas valve will open, which is why a weak igniter makes an oven slow to light.

## Induction
In 1820 Hans Christian Ørsted saw a compass needle swing near a wire carrying current: a current makes a **magnetic field**. A coil under an induction cooktop carries a rapidly alternating current, so its field flips back and forth thousands of times a second. In a pan of **iron** (or magnetic stainless steel), that changing field induces swirling **eddy currents**, and the pan's resistance turns them into heat. So the pan heats itself, and the glass top gets warm only from the pan. It's fast, easy to control, and far more efficient than gas; it needs pans a magnet will stick to.

## The microwave
A **magnetron** turns high-voltage electricity into microwaves (2.45 gigahertz), which a **waveguide** carries into the metal box. They make water molecules in the food jostle, and that motion is heat. Inside is a **high-voltage capacitor** that can hold a deadly charge even after unplugging, so the inside of a microwave is for a trained technician only; a countertop unit usually costs less to replace than to repair.

## The disposer
A disposer doesn't have blades: a spinning **plate** with swiveling **lugs** flings the food against a stationary **grind ring**, which shreds it small enough to wash away. Run cold water during and after grinding. Keep out stringy and starchy things (celery, corn husks, banana peels, potato peels), and grease. If a dishwasher drains into a new disposer, knock out the **plug** in its dishwasher inlet first, or the dishwasher can't drain.

## The refrigerator
![How a refrigerator moves heat out (drawn for this app)](figures/home/refrigerator.svg)
A refrigerator is a heat pump that moves heat from inside the box to the kitchen. The **compressor** pumps refrigerant to the **condenser coil**, under or behind the fridge, where it gives off heat; a thin **capillary tube** drops its pressure; and it boils in the **evaporator coil** behind the freezer wall, taking heat from the air that a fan blows over it into both compartments. A **defrost** heater melts the frost off the evaporator every so often, and the water drains to a pan underneath where it evaporates. Keep the fridge at about 37°F (at most 40°F) and the freezer at 0°F.

An **icemaker**: a **water valve** opens for a few seconds to fill a mold; when a thermostat senses the cubes are frozen, a small heater loosens them and an arm sweeps them into the bin; a wire **shut-off arm** stops it when the bin is full.

## The trash compactor
A motor turns two long screws that drive a **ram** down into the drawer, pressing the trash to a fraction of its size. It runs only with the drawer fully closed and the **key switch** on.

## Before calling for help
- **Anything dead**: check the breaker (switch it fully off and on) and that it's plugged in; for a dishwasher or disposer, a wall switch may be off.
- **Dishes not clean**: use dishwasher detergent, scrape off food, clean the filter and the spray-arm holes, and check that the water is hot (about 120 to 140°F; run the kitchen tap hot first).
- **Dishwasher leaks**: too much or the wrong detergent (dish soap foams over), a stuck float, or a dirty door gasket.
- **A coil or oven element won't heat**: it's probably burned out; it plugs in and can be replaced.
- **A gas burner won't light**: clear its small ports with a needle or a straightened paper clip. **An oven won't bake**: check whether it's set for a timed bake or self-clean.
- **A disposer hums but won't turn**: it's jammed. Turn it off, work the hex socket on the bottom back and forth with its wrench (or the flywheel with a broom handle from above), then press the red **reset** button. If water backs up while it runs, the clog is in the drain pipe.
- **A fridge cools poorly**: vacuum the condenser coils. If the freezer's back wall is caked with ice, the defrost system has failed: unplug it with the doors open for a day to melt it, and if that fixes it for a while, get the defrost part replaced.
- **No ice**: lower the shut-off arm; thaw an iced fill tube with a hair dryer; turn the freezer colder.
- **Compactor won't run**: check the key switch and that the drawer closes all the way; clean and oil a sticking drawer's rollers.
