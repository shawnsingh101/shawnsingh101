The machines built into a house and what they need: the range, refrigerator, dishwasher, disposal, washer, and the dryer and its exhaust duct.

## Kitchen
- An electric range or oven runs on a {{240-volt|what voltage?}} circuit; a gas one needs only a 120-volt outlet for its controls.
- A bracket on the floor behind a range, the {{anti-tip bracket|what?}}, keeps it from tipping over when a child stands on the open door.
- A refrigerator gives off the heat it removes through its {{condenser coils|what?}}, under or behind it, which should be dusted off now and then.
![A refrigerator](commons:Open refrigerator with food at night.jpg)
- A dishwasher's drain hose loops up high under the counter, or goes through an {{air gap|what?}} on the sink, so dirty water can't flow back in.
![A dishwasher](commons:Dishwasher with dishes.JPG)
- A {{garbage disposal|what?}} under the sink grinds food scraps so they wash down the drain.
![A garbage disposal](commons:InSinkEratorEvolution.jpg)
- A jammed disposal can be freed with a hex key in the socket on the bottom, then restarted with its {{reset button|what?}}.

## Laundry
- A washing machine's hot and cold hoses should be {{braided stainless steel|what kind?}}, which burst far less often than rubber.
![A washing machine](commons:Washing Machine Beko.jpg)
- An electric clothes dryer needs a {{240-volt|what voltage?}} circuit (usually 30 amps).
![A clothes dryer](commons:Whirlpool Cabrio Dryer.jpg)
- A dryer must vent outdoors through rigid or semi-rigid {{metal|what material?}} duct, as short and straight as possible.
- Clean a dryer's lint screen every load and its vent duct about once a {{year|how often?}}: lint buildup is a leading cause of dryer fires.
