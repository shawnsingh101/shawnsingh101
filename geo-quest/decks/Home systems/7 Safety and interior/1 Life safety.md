What keeps the people inside safe: smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, fire extinguishers, and a way out of every bedroom.

## Alarms
- A {{smoke alarm|what?}} should be in every bedroom, outside each sleeping area, and on every level of the house.
![A smoke alarm](commons:Smoke detector.JPG)
- A smoke alarm should be replaced every {{10 years|how often?}}; the date is printed on the back.
- When one smoke alarm sounds, {{interconnected|what kind?}} alarms all sound together, by wire or wirelessly.
- A {{photoelectric|which kind?}} smoke alarm is quicker to sense smoldering fires; an ionization alarm is quicker for flaming fires.
- A {{carbon monoxide alarm|what?}} belongs outside each sleeping area and on every level of a house with fuel-burning appliances or an attached garage.
![A carbon monoxide alarm](commons:Carbon monoxide detector 1 2018-03-01.jpg)
> Carbon monoxide has no color or smell; it comes from furnaces, water heaters, stoves, fireplaces, generators and cars.

## Fire extinguishers
- A home fire extinguisher labeled {{ABC|what rating?}} works on ordinary combustibles, flammable liquids and electrical fires.
- The classes of fire on an extinguisher's label:
```parts
A :: ordinary combustibles: wood, paper, cloth
B :: flammable liquids: gasoline, grease, oil
C :: energized electrical equipment
```
- How to use a fire extinguisher, in order:
```parts
P :: pull the pin
A :: aim at the base of the fire
S :: squeeze the handle
S :: sweep from side to side
```

## A way out
- An {{egress window|what?}} is a window big enough to escape through, required in every bedroom, including one in a basement.
- An egress window's sill may be no higher than {{44 inches|how high?}} above the floor.
- An egress window must open to at least {{5.7 square feet|how big?}} (5.0 on the ground floor).
