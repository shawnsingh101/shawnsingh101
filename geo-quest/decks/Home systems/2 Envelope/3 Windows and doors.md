title: Windows and doors
---
The openings in the envelope: window types and their parts, glass, skylights, exterior doors, and the seals around them.

## Window types
- A {{double-hung|which type?}} window has two sashes that both slide up and down.
- A {{casement|which type?}} window is hinged at the side and cranks outward.
- A window whose sash slides sideways in a track is a {{slider|which type?}}.
- A window that doesn't open at all is a {{fixed|which type?}} window (also called a picture window).

## Window parts
- The {{sash|which part?}} is the frame that holds the glass and moves when the window opens.
- Two or three panes sealed together with a gas-filled gap between them make {{insulated glass|what?}} (double or triple glazing).
- A thin metal coating on window glass that reflects heat back where it came from is called {{low-E (low-emissivity)|what?}}.
- Fog between the panes of a double-glazed window means its {{seal|what?}} has failed.

## Roof windows and doors
- A {{skylight|what?}} is a window set into the roof.
![A skylight](commons:Velux GGL-7 Center-Pivot Roof Window, 1986 Model.jpg)
- Most exterior doors today are steel, {{fiberglass|what material?}} or wood.
- The strip under an exterior door, on the floor, is its {{threshold|which part?}}.

## Sealing the gaps
- {{Weatherstripping|what?}} seals the gaps around the moving parts of a door or window.
- {{Caulk|what?}} seals the fixed joints, like where a window frame meets the siding.
