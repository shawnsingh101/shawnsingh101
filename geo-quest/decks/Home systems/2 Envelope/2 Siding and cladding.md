The outside face of the walls: what cladding does, the common sidings, and masonry and stucco.

## What cladding does
- Siding sheds most of the rain, but it isn't watertight: the layer behind it, the {{house wrap|which layer?}}, stops the water that gets past.
- A gap behind siding that lets water drain and the wall dry out is a {{rainscreen|what?}}.

## Sidings
- {{Vinyl siding|which siding?}}, made of PVC plastic, is the most common siding on American houses.
- {{Fiber-cement siding|which siding?}}, made of cement, sand and wood fibers, resists fire, rot and termites.
- Overlapping horizontal boards of wood siding are called {{clapboards|what?}}.

## Masonry and plaster
- On most brick houses today, the brick is a {{veneer|what?}}: one layer tied to a wood-framed wall, with an air gap behind it, carrying no weight but its own.
![A brick house](commons:Brick veneer residential construction.jpg)
- {{Stucco|what?}} is a cement plaster applied in coats over metal lath on the outside of a wall.
- Thin cut stone, or cast stone made to look like it, applied to the face of a wall is {{stone veneer|what?}}.
![Stone veneer](commons:Stone Clad House Greater Manchester.jpg)
- Small holes or gaps left in the bottom course of a brick veneer wall, to let water out, are {{weep holes|what?}}.
