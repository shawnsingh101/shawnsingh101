What keeps heat in and air and moisture out: R-value, the kinds of insulation, house wrap, air barriers and vapor retarders.

## How it's rated
- Insulation is rated by its {{R-value|what rating?}}, its resistance to heat flow: the higher, the better it insulates.
- An attic in a cold US climate is usually insulated to about {{R-49 to R-60|what R-value?}}.
> The Department of Energy's recommendations rise from about R-30 in the South to R-60 in the far North.

## Kinds of insulation
- {{Batts|which form?}} are precut blankets of fiberglass or mineral wool, fitted between studs and joists.
![Fiberglass batts](commons:Glass wool insulation.jpg)
- {{Loose-fill|which form?}} insulation (fiberglass or cellulose) is blown into attics and closed wall cavities.
![Blown-in insulation in an attic](commons:Cellulose insulation300.jpg)
- {{Cellulose|which insulation?}} is made from recycled paper treated to resist fire.
- {{Spray foam|which insulation?}} goes on as a liquid that expands and hardens, insulating and sealing air leaks in one step.
![Spray foam insulation](commons:Spray Polyurethane Foam Application.jpg)
- Closed-cell spray foam is denser, insulates more per inch and blocks moisture; {{open-cell|which kind?}} foam is softer and lets vapor through.
- Rigid insulation boards (foam board) are made of polystyrene or {{polyiso|what?}} (polyisocyanurate).

## Air and moisture
- {{House wrap|what?}} is a sheet over the wall sheathing that stops wind and liquid water but lets water vapor out.
![House wrap on a new house](commons:Tyvek house wrap.jpg)
- An {{air barrier|what?}} stops air from flowing through the envelope; air leaks carry far more heat and moisture than the walls conduct.
- A {{vapor retarder|what?}} (a plastic sheet, or the kraft-paper facing on batts) slows moisture soaking into a wall.
- In a cold climate the vapor retarder goes on the {{warm side|which side?}} of the insulation, toward the inside of the house.
> Warm indoor air holds moisture; if it reaches a cold surface inside the wall, it condenses there.
