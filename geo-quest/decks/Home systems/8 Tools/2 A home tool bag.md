A basic kit beyond the first Tools deck: the pliers, wrenches, saws, smoothing and measuring tools, and safety gear that handle most household repairs. Each tool shows its picture to name, then what it's for. The list follows appendix D of *How Your House Works* (Charlie Wing), leaving out the tools already in Tools; the cards are our own.

:::lesson
## A kit that does most jobs
Most household repairs need a few tools of each kind, not a workshop:
- **Gripping and turning**: slip-joint, groove-joint, needle-nose and locking pliers; an adjustable wrench, combination wrenches, a socket set and hex keys (inch and metric sizes); a clamp or two.
- **Drilling and cutting**: a drill with twist bits (for wood, metal and plastic) and spade bits (big holes in wood), a hacksaw for metal, a crosscut handsaw, a utility knife and wood chisels.
- **Fastening and smoothing**: a multi-bit screwdriver, a claw hammer and nail sets, a staple gun, a sander, putty knives and a file.
- **Measuring and electrical**: a tape measure, framing and speed squares, a level, a stud finder, lineman's pliers, wire strippers and a multimeter.
- **Everything else**: a pry bar, an extension cord, safety glasses, a flashlight and a pair of sawhorses, carried in a canvas tool bag that won't rust or scratch floors.

Buy good quality in the tools you'll use most; the rest can be inexpensive.
