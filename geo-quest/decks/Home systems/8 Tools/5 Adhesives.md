Which glue for which job: how wood glue, construction adhesive, contact cement, hot glue, epoxy, polyurethane glue, super glue and silicone each set, how strong and how waterproof they are, and what each is best for. The topics follow appendix C of *How Your House Works* (Charlie Wing); the cards are our own.

## How glues set
There is no all-purpose glue. Each sets in one of four ways, and that decides how it's used:
- **Drying** (the water or solvent evaporates): wood glue, construction adhesive, contact cement, spray adhesive.
- **Cooling**: hot glue.
- **Reacting with a hardener**: two-part epoxy.
- **Reacting with moisture**: polyurethane glue, super glue (cyanoacrylate) and silicone.
Before choosing, read the label for four things: **water resistance** (indoors or out), whether it needs **clamping**, how long until **full strength**, and whether it stays **flexible**.

| Glue | Best for | Clamp? | Fills gaps? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wood (PVA) glue | wood joints, indoors | yes | no |
| Construction adhesive | subfloors, paneling, trim | no (nail or screw) | yes |
| Contact cement | laminate, veneer, rubber | no: bonds on contact | no |
| Hot glue | crafts, quick light holds | no | yes |
| Epoxy | metal, glass, ceramics, repairs | no | yes |
| Polyurethane glue | wood outdoors, mixed materials | yes | foams |
| Super glue | small, tight-fitting parts | no | no |
| Silicone | wet places that move | no | yes |

## The everyday glues
- **Wood glue** (white or yellow, polyvinyl acetate, PVA) soaks into the wood; a tight, clamped joint ends up stronger than the wood around it. Clamp for about an hour and let it cure a day. Water cleans it up while wet; for outdoors use a water-resistant type.
- **Construction adhesive**, squeezed from a caulk gun, fills gaps and bonds subfloors to joists (stopping squeaks), paneling, foam board and trim, usually along with nails or screws.
- **Contact cement** is brushed or rolled on **both** surfaces and left to dry to the touch; when they meet, they bond instantly and can't be shifted, so line them up with strips of wood between them, then slide the strips out.
- **Hot glue** melts in a glue gun and sets in seconds as it cools: handy, but weak and softened by heat.

## The strong ones
- **Two-part epoxy**: a resin and a hardener, mixed just before use; it fills gaps, sticks to almost anything, and is strong and waterproof. Quick-set (five-minute) epoxy is handy; slow-set is stronger.
- **Polyurethane glue** cures with moisture (dampen one surface), is waterproof, and foams as it sets, so clamp the parts.
- **Super glue** (cyanoacrylate) sets in seconds with the trace of moisture on surfaces, for small, tight-fitting parts; it bonds skin just as fast.
- **Silicone** cures with moisture from the air into a flexible, waterproof rubber, for joints around tubs and sinks that move; paint won't stick to it.
