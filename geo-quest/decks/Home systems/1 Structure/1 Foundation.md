The skeleton of a house, from the ground up: what the foundation is, the three kinds of foundation, and how the weight of the house reaches the soil. Written in our own words from Wikipedia and the International Residential Code's basics.

:::lesson
## What the structure does
A house's structural system holds up its own weight (the **dead load**), everything in it (the **live load**: people, furniture), and what weather adds (**snow** on the roof, **wind** on the walls), and the foundation walls also hold back the **soil** pressing in from outside. Every one of those forces travels down through the frame until the foundation spreads it into the ground.

```stack
# How the load reaches the ground
6 | Roof | rafters or trusses
5 | Walls | studs, with headers over openings
4 | Floor | joists on the sill plate
3 | Foundation wall | concrete or block
2 | Footing | spreads the load
1 | Soil | carries it all
```

## Three kinds of foundation
- **Slab-on-grade**: one concrete slab poured on the ground; common in warm climates.
- **Crawlspace**: short walls or piers lift the floor a few feet off the ground.
- **Basement**: full-height walls make a story below ground; common where footings must go deep anyway to get below the frost line.
:::

## The base under the walls
- A {{footing|which part?}} is the wide concrete base under a foundation wall or post: it spreads the weight over more soil.
- In cold climates, footings go below the {{frost line|what depth?}}, so soil that freezes and swells can't lift them.
> Frost depth runs from about a foot in the South to four feet or more in the northern states; the local building code sets it.
- Steel reinforcing bars in a footing or foundation wall are called {{rebar|what?}}.

## Three kinds of foundation
- The three common kinds of house foundation, shallowest first:
```parts
poured right on the ground :: slab-on-grade
floor lifted a few feet on short walls :: crawlspace
a full story below ground :: basement
```
- A {{slab-on-grade|which foundation?}} is a concrete floor poured directly on the ground, usually thickened at the edges to act as its own footing.
![Forms for a concrete slab](commons:Concrete Slab Forms .jpg)
- A {{crawlspace|which foundation?}} lifts the floor on short walls or piers, leaving a space under the house for pipes, ducts and wires.
![A crawlspace access panel](commons:Crawlspace access panel.jpg)
- {{Basement|which foundation?}} walls, of poured concrete or concrete block, carry the house and also hold back the soil outside.
- The sideways push of wet soil against a basement wall is called {{hydrostatic pressure|what?}}.
> It is why basement walls crack and leak when the ground around them stays soaked: see Drainage and grading.

## Tying the house down
- {{Anchor bolts|what?}} set in the top of the foundation wall hold the house's wooden frame to the concrete.
![Anchor bolts in a foundation](commons:Anchor bolt in concrete.jpg)
- Wood that touches concrete or masonry, like the sill plate, must be {{pressure-treated|how treated?}} so it resists rot and termites.
