title: Framing
---
The wooden frame that stands on the foundation: sill plate, floor joists and subfloor, wall studs and headers, ceiling joists, and the rafters or trusses that hold up the roof.

## How it's built
- Most American houses are built with {{platform framing|which method?}}: each floor is built as a deck, and the next story's walls stand on it.
> Before about 1940, many houses used balloon framing instead: studs ran unbroken from the sill to the roof.
- Most house framing is softwood lumber in standard sizes: a "2×4" actually measures {{1½ by 3½ inches|how big?}}.

## The floor
- The {{sill plate|which board?}} is the board bolted flat on top of the foundation wall; the wood frame starts on it.
- {{Floor joists|which members?}} are the parallel boards, set on edge, that span between walls or beams and carry the floor.
- The {{rim joist|which member?}} (band joist) closes off the ends of the floor joists around the edge of the floor.
- {{Subflooring|what?}}, sheets of plywood or OSB nailed across the joists, is the base under the finished floor.

## The walls
- {{Wall studs|which members?}} are the vertical members of a wood-framed wall, usually 2×4s or 2×6s.
- Wall studs are usually spaced {{16 inches|how far apart?}} on center (sometimes 24).
- Studs stand on a {{bottom plate|which board?}} (sole plate) and are capped by a doubled top plate.
- A {{header|which member?}} spans the top of a window or door opening and carries the weight above it to the studs on each side.
- A {{load-bearing wall|which kind of wall?}} carries weight from above; a partition wall only divides rooms.
> Before removing any wall, find out which kind it is: interior walls that run across the joists, near the middle of the house, are often load-bearing.

## The roof
- {{Ceiling joists|which members?}} carry the ceiling under the attic and tie the bottoms of the rafters together so the roof can't push the walls apart.
- {{Rafters|which members?}} are sloped boards, cut on site, that run from the top of the wall up to the ridge.
![Roof rafters](commons:Photograph of the Roof Framing in the Bequet-Ribault House in Ste Genevieve MO.png)
- {{Roof trusses|what?}} are triangulated frames made in a factory; they carry the roof and ceiling together and span from wall to wall.
> Trusses go up fast and use smaller lumber, but their webs fill the attic: never cut a truss member to make room.
