The yard machines in depth: how four-stroke and two-stroke engines and their carburetors work, what's inside a chain saw, a mower, a string trimmer and a leaf blower, battery-powered versions of each, and the fixes to try before taking one to the shop. Order follows chapter 10 of *How Your House Works* (Charlie Wing); the cards are our own. Always follow the machine's own manual for its fuel mix, settings and safety rules.

:::lesson
## The four-stroke engine
![A four-stroke engine running](commons:4StrokeEngine Ortho 3D Small.gif)
A piston slides in a cylinder, linked by a **connecting rod** to the **crankshaft**, which turns its up-and-down motion into rotation. One cycle takes four strokes, two turns of the crankshaft:
1. **Intake**: the piston moves down with the intake **valve** open, drawing in a mix of fuel and air.
2. **Compression**: both valves shut and the piston squeezes the mix.
3. **Power**: the **spark plug** fires, the burning mix expands and drives the piston down.
4. **Exhaust**: the piston rises with the exhaust valve open and pushes out the burned gas.

**Cams** turned by the crankshaft open the valves at the right moments, and the oil sits in the **crankcase** below, splashed or pumped onto the moving parts; it is checked with a dipstick and changed every season. A **governor** holds the engine's speed steady as the load changes, so a mower doesn't slow in thick grass.

## The carburetor
The carburetor mixes fuel into the air. A **float** in a small bowl works a needle valve, like a toilet's fill valve, to keep the fuel at a steady level. Air rushing through a narrow throat (a **venturi**) draws fuel from a jet; the **throttle** plate sets how much mix goes in, and so the power; the **choke** plate partly closes the air intake to give a cold engine the richer mix it needs to start. A **primer bulb** pushes a squirt of fuel in for a cold start instead.

## The two-stroke engine
![A two-stroke engine running](commons:Two-Stroke Engine.gif)
A two-stroke engine fires once every turn. It has no valves: the piston itself uncovers **ports** in the cylinder wall. As the piston rises, it compresses the charge above it and draws fresh mix into the crankcase below; as it is driven down, it pushes that mix up through a transfer port while the burned gas leaves through the exhaust port. With no oil sump, the oil comes **mixed in the fuel**, commonly 50 parts gas to 1 of two-stroke oil (2.6 ounces per gallon), but use the maker's ratio. Simple, light and happy at any angle, it suits a chain saw or trimmer; but some fresh mix escapes with the exhaust and the burned oil smokes, so emission rules keep pushing these engines toward four-stroke and battery designs.

## The chain saw
A loop of **chain** with sharp cutting teeth runs around a **guide bar**, driven by a sprocket. A **centrifugal clutch** engages only when the engine speeds up, so at idle the chain should stand still. An oil pump keeps the bar and chain lubricated from their own tank of **bar oil**. The danger is **kickback**: if the upper part of the bar's tip catches the wood, the bar flies up and back toward the user. A **chain brake**, tripped by the front hand guard or by the saw's own jerk, stops the chain in a fraction of a second. Wear eye and ear protection and chaps, and keep the chain sharp and properly tensioned: snug on the bar but still easy to pull around by hand.

The carburetor has three screws: **H** (fuel at high speed), **L** (fuel at low speed) and **LA** or **T** (idle speed).

## The lawn mower
The **blade** bolts straight to the bottom of the engine's vertical crankshaft. Its edges are shaped to lift the grass upright as it cuts, and the turned-up back edges (**sails**) blow the clippings out the side chute, into the bag, or around under the deck to be chopped fine (**mulching**). On a **self-propelled** mower, a belt from the crankshaft drives the wheels through a gearbox; squeezing the bail tightens the belt. A dull blade tears grass and leaves brown tips: sharpen it once or twice a season.

## String trimmers, blowers and cordless tools
A string trimmer spins nylon **line** from a spool in its head; on most, tapping the head on the ground (**bump feed**) lets out more line. A leaf blower is a small engine turning an **impeller**, a centrifugal fan. **Cordless** versions of all these tools, with **brushless** motors and lithium-ion packs, are now strong enough for most yards: no fuel to go stale, no pull cord, little noise. Store their batteries partly charged in a cool, dry place, and don't charge them below freezing.

## Before calling for help
- **Won't start**: is there fuel (gas in the gas tank, not the oil fill)? Is it fresh? Gas more than a couple of months old should be drained and replaced. Is the choke set for a cold start, and the switch on?
- **Smells of gas and won't start**: it's flooded. Remove the spark plug, dry it, pull the cord several times with the switch off to clear the cylinder, put the plug back and try again with no choke.
- **Worn or fouled plug**: replace it with the same type, gapped as the manual says. Clean or replace a dirty **air filter**.
- **Chain saw chain turns at idle**: turn the idle screw (LA) counterclockwise until it stops, then a little more. **Stalls at idle**: turn it clockwise until the chain just starts to move, then back off a quarter turn.
- **Mower cuts poorly or vibrates**: the blade is dull or bent. Pull off the spark plug wire, tip the mower with the air filter and carburetor up, block the blade with a piece of wood, and loosen the bolt; put the new blade on with its sails pointing up into the deck.
- **Self-propelled mower won't climb**: the drive belt is worn or loose; replace it with the same part number.
- **Trimmer line won't feed**: rewind the spool with new line of the right diameter, in the direction of the arrow.
- **Before storing for the season**: add stabilizer and run it through, or run the tank dry; put in a new plug and a clean filter at the start of the next season.
