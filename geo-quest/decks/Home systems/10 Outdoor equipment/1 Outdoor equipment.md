The machines outside the house: small gasoline engines and what keeps them starting, battery-powered yard tools, a generator or battery for power outages and how to use it safely, the lawn sprinklers and the pool pump.

:::lesson
## Small engines
Mowers, trimmers, chain saws and blowers run on small gasoline engines of two kinds. A **four-stroke** engine (most mowers and generators) keeps its oil in the crankcase, like a car. A **two-stroke** engine (most chain saws, trimmers and blowers) is lighter and runs in any position, but its oil is **mixed into the gasoline**. Today's gasoline, with 10% ethanol, goes stale in a couple of months and gums up small engines, so use fresh fuel, add a **fuel stabilizer** to anything that will sit, and run tanks dry before storing.

## Power in an outage
A gasoline **generator** makes carbon monoxide that can kill in minutes: run it **outdoors only**, at least 20 feet from the house, never in a garage, even with the door open. Never plug it into a house outlet; connect it to the house only through a **transfer switch** or an approved **interlock** on the panel, put in by an electrician. A battery **power station** makes no fumes and can run indoors, but holds much less energy.
