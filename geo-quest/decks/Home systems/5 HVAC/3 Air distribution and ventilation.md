title: Air distribution and ventilation
---
How heated or cooled air gets around, and how stale air gets out: ducts, registers and returns, filters, the thermostat, exhaust fans and balanced ventilation.

## Ducts
- {{Supply ducts|which ducts?}} carry heated or cooled air from the furnace or air handler to the rooms.
- Air comes into a room through a {{register|what?}}, a grille with dampers that can be opened or closed.
![A floor register](commons:Floor Register.jpg)
- Air goes back to the furnace through a {{return grille|what?}}, usually larger and with no damper.
- The box on the furnace that the main ducts connect to is the {{plenum|what?}}.

## Cleaning and control
- The furnace's {{air filter|what?}} sits where the return air enters and should be checked every one to three months.
![A furnace filter](commons:HVAC air filter.webp)
- A furnace filter's rating for how fine a particle it catches, from 1 to 16, is its {{MERV (minimum efficiency reporting value)|which rating?}}.
- The {{thermostat|what?}} turns the heating and cooling on and off to hold the temperature you set.
![A thermostat](commons:A touch-screen programmable thermostat.jpg)

## Exhaust fans
- A {{bathroom exhaust fan|what?}} pulls out moist air; it must vent outdoors, never just into the attic.
- Over the stove, a {{range hood|what?}} pulls out smoke, grease and cooking moisture.
![A range hood](commons:Extractor-hood.jpg)

## Fresh air
- A tight house needs fresh air brought in; a {{heat recovery ventilator|what?}} (HRV) brings it in while passing the heat of the outgoing stale air to it.
- An {{energy recovery ventilator|what?}} (ERV) passes moisture across as well as heat.
