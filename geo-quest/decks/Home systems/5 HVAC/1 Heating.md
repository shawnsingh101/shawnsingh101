title: Heating

How a house is heated: forced-air furnaces, boilers with radiators or hydronic baseboard, and heat pumps.

## Forced air
- A {{furnace|what?}} heats air, by burning gas or oil or with electric elements, and a blower pushes it through ducts to the rooms.
![A forced-air furnace](commons:Lamneck-central-heating-gas-furnace-cutaway-diagram.png)
- Inside a gas furnace, the {{heat exchanger|which part?}} keeps the flame's exhaust apart from the air going to the rooms.
> A cracked heat exchanger can leak carbon monoxide into the house: one reason to have a furnace checked every year.
- A furnace's efficiency is rated by its {{AFUE (annual fuel utilization efficiency)|which rating?}}: the share of the fuel's heat that reaches the house.
- A high-efficiency furnace (90% or more) cools its exhaust so much that it can vent through {{plastic pipe|what kind of pipe?}} instead of a chimney.

## Hot-water heat
- A {{boiler|what?}} heats water (or makes steam) and sends it through pipes to radiators or baseboards.
- Heating with water circulated through pipes is called {{hydronic|what kind of?}} heat.
- An old cast-iron {{radiator|what?}} gives off heat from hot water or steam inside it.
![A cast-iron radiator](commons:Household radiator.jpg)
- Low metal units with fins along the bottom of a wall, heated by water from a boiler, are {{baseboard heaters|what?}}.
![Hydronic baseboard](commons:Hotwater baseboard radiator (2).JPG)
- Warm-water tubing laid under the floor is {{radiant floor|what kind of?}} heat.

## Moving heat
- A {{heat pump|what?}} doesn't make heat; it moves heat, pulling it from the outdoor air in winter and running in reverse to cool in summer.
![A heat pump's outdoor unit](commons:Large heat pump setup 2.webp)
- Because it moves heat instead of making it, a heat pump can deliver {{two to four|how many?}} units of heat for each unit of electricity it uses.
- A {{geothermal|which kind?}} (ground-source) heat pump draws heat from pipes buried in the ground instead of from the air.
