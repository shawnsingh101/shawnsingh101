title: Cooling

How central air, heat pumps and mini-splits cool a house: the refrigerant loop, the outdoor condenser and indoor coil, and the water they wring out of the air.

:::lesson
## The refrigerant loop
An air conditioner moves heat from inside to outside with a **refrigerant** that circulates in a closed loop:
```stack
# Around the loop
4 | Expansion valve | pressure drops; refrigerant gets very cold
3 | Condenser coil (outside) | gives off the heat; refrigerant turns back to liquid
2 | Compressor (outside) | squeezes the gas; it gets hot
1 | Evaporator coil (inside) | absorbs the house's heat; refrigerant boils to gas
```
The indoor coil also cools air below its dew point, so water drips off it: that's how air conditioning dries the air.
:::

## Central air
- The {{condenser|which unit?}} is the outdoor unit of a central air system: compressor, coil and a big fan that blow the house's heat outside.
![A central air condenser](commons:Condenser unit for central air conditioning.JPG)
- The {{evaporator coil|which part?}} sits on top of the furnace or air handler and absorbs heat from the house air blown across it.
- The two copper pipes that join the indoor and outdoor units carry the {{refrigerant|what?}}.
- The {{compressor|which part?}}, in the outdoor unit, pumps the refrigerant around the loop.
- Water that drips off the indoor coil is {{condensate|what?}}; it runs out through a drain line or a small pump.
- A central air conditioner's efficiency is rated by its {{SEER (seasonal energy efficiency ratio)|which rating?}}.

## Other ways to cool
- A heat pump can cool or heat because a {{reversing valve|which part?}} switches the direction the refrigerant flows.
- A {{mini-split|what?}} has an outdoor unit and one or more wall-mounted indoor units joined by refrigerant lines, with no ductwork.
