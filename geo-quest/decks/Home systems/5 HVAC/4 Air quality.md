Keeping the air inside healthy: the right humidity, where condensation and mold come from, and the machines that add or take out moisture and clean the air.

## Humidity
- The amount of water vapor in the air, as a share of the most it could hold at that temperature, is its {{relative humidity|what?}}.
- Indoor relative humidity is best kept between about {{30 and 50%|what range?}}, and below 60%.
- The temperature at which air can hold no more water vapor, so water condenses out of it, is its {{dew point|what?}}.
- Water on the inside of windows in winter means warm, moist room air is being cooled below its {{dew point|what?}} on the cold glass.

## Mold
- Mold grows where it's warm enough and the humidity stays high, usually above about {{70%|how high?}} at the surface.
- In a heated house, mold shows up on the {{coldest|warmest or coldest?}} surfaces: outside corners, closets on outside walls, and the attic.
- The key to stopping mold is to control {{moisture|what?}}: fix leaks, vent baths, kitchens and dryers outdoors, and dry out damp basements.

## Machines
- A {{humidifier|what?}} adds moisture to dry winter air.

- A {{dehumidifier|what?}} takes moisture out of the air, often in a damp basement.
