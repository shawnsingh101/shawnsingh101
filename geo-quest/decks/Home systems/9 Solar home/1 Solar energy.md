Using the sun at home: passive solar design, solar water heating, and solar electric panels, how they connect to the house and the grid, and where they work best.

:::lesson
## Three ways to use the sun
- **Passive solar**: the house itself collects heat, through south-facing windows, and stores it in masonry, with no pumps or fans.
- **Solar thermal**: collectors on the roof heat water for the taps or the pool.
- **Photovoltaic (PV)**: panels turn sunlight straight into electricity.

## Solar electricity in one picture
```stack
# From sunlight to the outlet
5 | Outlets | the house uses the power first
4 | Main panel | the inverter feeds a breaker
3 | Inverter | turns DC into 120/240-volt AC
2 | Panels | cells make direct current (DC)
1 | Sunlight | strongest when it strikes the panel square on
```
Power the house doesn't use flows out to the grid, and under **net metering** the meter credits it. In the Northern Hemisphere, panels do best facing **south**, tilted about as steeply as the local latitude, and **out of shade**: even a little shade cuts output a lot.
:::
