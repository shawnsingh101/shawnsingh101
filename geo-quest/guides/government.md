# Government

- **By country**: `decks/Government/<n> <Country>/…`. The United States first: Foundations, then each branch and
  department; the State Department is built out first.
- **History of government stays in History**; **Law** will be its own subject (the Constitution stays here). A law
  that matters to both is filed once and shown in the other with `also in:`.
- **Durable vs. current**: structure, law and treaties are ordinary cards. People in office, budgets and stated
  priorities carry `as of:` and sit in a deck that `follows:` Wikipedia, so the weekly briefing keeps them current.
  Never mix the two in one card.
- **Neutral and attributed**: an administration's goals in its own words, with whose words they are.
- **Not FSO preparation**, as with Economics.
- **Sources**: US government works are public domain (state.gov, the Foreign Affairs Manual, CRS reports); check
  current facts against state.gov and Wikipedia.
- **Titles are spelled as the office spells them**, checked against the office's own page or its Wikipedia article:
  the *National Security Advisor*, but the State Department's *Legal Adviser*. Not from memory or a style guide.
